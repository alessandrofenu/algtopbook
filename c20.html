Chapter 20

Adjunctions, Kan extensions, and nerve–realisation

In this chapter

  1. 20.1 Adjunctions
  2. 20.2 Kan extensions
  3. 20.3 Density
  4. 20.4 Nerve and realisation
  5. 20.5 What to carry forward

Part IV changes the level of description. Until now a construction was given by a formula and its properties proved by inspection; from here on constructions are characterised by universal properties, and their properties are consequences of those characterisations rather than of the formulas.

The gain is not elegance but transferability. Geometric realisation, the nerve of a category, the classifying space of a group, the simplicial chain complex — all are instances of one construction, and a proof given once at the general level covers all of them. This chapter sets up that construction. Chapters 21 and 22 then redo the homotopy theory of Parts I–III in the same language.

20.1 Adjunctions

Definition 20.1. Functors F ⁣:C→DF\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}F:C→D and G ⁣:D→CG\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}G:D→C are adjoint, written F⊣GF\dashv GF⊣G, if there are bijections

D(Fc,d) ≅ C(c,Gd)\mathcal{D}\big(Fc,d\big)\ \cong\ \mathcal{C}\big(c,Gd\big)D(Fc,d) ≅ C(c,Gd)

natural in ccc and ddd. Equivalently, there are a unit η ⁣:id⇒GF\eta\colon\mathrm{id}\Rightarrow GFη:id⇒GF and a counit ε ⁣:FG⇒id\varepsilon\colon FG\Rightarrow\mathrm{id}ε:FG⇒id satisfying the triangle identities.

Exercise 20.2 (easy). Show that a left adjoint preserves all colimits that exist.

Hint. Compare the two sets of maps out of a colimit into a fixed object, using the adjunction to move FFF across.

Solution. Let D ⁣:J→CD\colon J\to\mathcal{C}D:J→C have a colimit. For any d∈Dd\in\mathcal{D}d∈D,

D(F(colim D),d)≅C(colim D,Gd)≅lim⁡JC(Dj,Gd)≅lim⁡JD(FDj,d)≅D(colimJFDj, d),\mathcal{D}\big(F(\mathrm{colim}\,D),d\big)\cong \mathcal{C}\big(\mathrm{colim}\,D,Gd\big)\cong \lim_J\mathcal{C}\big(Dj,Gd\big)\cong \lim_J\mathcal{D}\big(FDj,d\big)\cong \mathcal{D}\big(\mathrm{colim}_JFDj,\ d\big),D(F(colimD),d)≅C(colimD,Gd)≅Jlim​C(Dj,Gd)≅Jlim​D(FDj,d)≅D(colimJ​FDj, d),

naturally in ddd; by Yoneda the two objects agree.

Warning 20.3. A left adjoint preserves colimits, and in general nothing else. Products are limits, so a left adjoint has no reason to preserve them, and Chapter 29 exhibits a left adjoint of importance that does not.

20.2 Kan extensions

Definition 20.4. Let K ⁣:D→EK\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}K:D→E and F ⁣:D→CF\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}F:D→C. A left Kan extension of FFF along KKK is a functor Lan⁡KF ⁣:E→C\Lan_KF\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{C}LanK​F:E→C together with a natural transformation η ⁣:F⇒(Lan⁡KF)K\eta\colon F\Rightarrow(\Lan_KF)Kη:F⇒(LanK​F)K that is universal: any γ ⁣:F⇒GK\gamma\colon F\Rightarrow GKγ:F⇒GK factors as γ=(δK)∘η\gamma=(\delta K)\circ\etaγ=(δK)∘η for a unique δ ⁣:Lan⁡KF⇒G\delta\colon\Lan_KF\Rightarrow Gδ:LanK​F⇒G.

Proposition 20.5 (Pointwise formula; recalled). Suppose D\mathcal{D}D is small and C\mathcal{C}C is cocomplete. Then Lan⁡KF\Lan_KFLanK​F exists, and

(Lan⁡KF)(e)=colim⁡(K↓e)F,\big(\Lan_KF\big)(e)=\operatorname*{colim}_{(K\downarrow e)}F ,(LanK​F)(e)=(K↓e)colim​F,

the colimit being taken over the comma category whose objects are the pairs consisting of d∈Dd\in\mathcal{D}d∈D and a map Kd→eKd\to eKd→e.

Example 20.6 (Colimits are Kan extensions). Take E=1\mathcal{E}=\mathbf{1}E=1, the terminal category. Then (K↓∗)(K\downarrow\ast)(K↓∗) is all of D\mathcal{D}D, and Proposition 20.5 reads Lan⁡KF=colim⁡DF\Lan_KF=\operatorname*{colim}_{\mathcal{D}}FLanK​F=colimD​F. So a colimit is the simplest kind of Kan extension, and conversely a Kan extension is a colimit computed one object at a time.

Definition 20.7. A left Kan extension is absolute if it is preserved by every functor out of C\mathcal{C}C: for every H ⁣:C→C′H\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}'H:C→C′, the pair (HLan⁡KF, Hη)(H\Lan_KF,\,H\eta)(HLanK​F,Hη) is a left Kan extension of HFHFHF along KKK.

Exercise 20.8 (easy). Show that if Lan⁡KF\Lan_KFLanK​F is absolute and Lan⁡K′(Lan⁡KF)\Lan_{K'}(\Lan_KF)LanK′​(LanK​F) is absolute, then the composite is an absolute left Kan extension of FFF along K′KK'KK′K.

Solution. Absoluteness of Lan⁡KF\Lan_KFLanK​F makes HLan⁡KFH\Lan_KFHLanK​F a left Kan extension of HFHFHF for every HHH; taking H=Lan⁡K′H=\Lan_{K'}H=LanK′​-composites and using the universal property twice, a transformation F⇒GK′KF\Rightarrow G K'KF⇒GK′K corresponds first to Lan⁡KF⇒GK′\Lan_KF\Rightarrow GK'LanK​F⇒GK′ and then to Lan⁡K′Lan⁡KF⇒G\Lan_{K'}\Lan_KF\Rightarrow GLanK′​LanK​F⇒G, each step uniquely. The composite therefore has the universal property of Lan⁡K′KF\Lan_{K'K}FLanK′K​F, and it is absolute because each stage is preserved by every functor.

20.3 Density

Presheaves are colimits of representables, and that single fact is what makes the next section work.

Theorem 20.9 (Density). Let D\mathcal{D}D be small and let Y ⁣:D→[Dop,Set]\mathcal{Y}\colon\mathcal{D}\to [\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}]Y:D→[Dop,Set] be the Yoneda embedding. Every presheaf XXX is canonically the colimit of representables:

X ≅ colim⁡∫X Y,X\ \cong\ \operatorname*{colim}_{\int X}\ \mathcal{Y},X ≅ ∫Xcolim​ Y,

where ∫X\int X∫X denotes the category of elements: its objects are the pairs (d,x)(d,x)(d,x) with x∈X(d)x\in X(d)x∈X(d).

Exercise 20.10 (medium). Prove Theorem 20.9.

Hint. Compute maps out of the colimit into an arbitrary presheaf ZZZ, and use the Yoneda lemma on each term.

Solution. For any presheaf ZZZ,

[Dop,Set](colim⁡∫XY, Z)≅lim⁡∫X[Dop,Set](Yd,Z)≅lim⁡(d,x)∈∫XZ(d),\big[\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}\big] \Big(\operatorname*{colim}_{\int X}\mathcal{Y},\ Z\Big) \cong\lim_{\int X}\big[\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}\big] \big(\mathcal{Y}d,Z\big) \cong\lim_{(d,x)\in\int X}Z(d),[Dop,Set](∫Xcolim​Y, Z)≅∫Xlim​[Dop,Set](Yd,Z)≅(d,x)∈∫Xlim​Z(d),

the last step by the Yoneda lemma. An element of that limit is a choice, for each x∈X(d)x\in X(d)x∈X(d), of an element of Z(d)Z(d)Z(d), compatible with the maps of ∫X\int X∫X — which is exactly a natural transformation X⇒ZX\Rightarrow ZX⇒Z. So the colimit represents the same functor as XXX, and Yoneda identifies them.

Remark 20.11 (Coend notation). The colimit of Theorem 20.9 is written as a coend,

X ≅ ∫d∈DX(d)⋅Yd,X\ \cong\ \int^{d\in\mathcal{D}}X(d)\cdot\mathcal{Y}d ,X ≅ ∫d∈DX(d)⋅Yd,

where S⋅cS\cdot cS⋅c denotes the coproduct of SSS copies of ccc. The notation is worth adopting because the formula it abbreviates is the one that appears in Section 20.4: replace Yd\mathcal{Y}dYd by an object of some other category and the same expression defines a realisation.

20.4 Nerve and realisation

Construction 20.12. Let D\mathcal{D}D be small, C\mathcal{C}C cocomplete, and F ⁣:D→CF\colon\mathcal{D}\to \mathcal{C}F:D→C any functor. Define

Sing⁡F ⁣:C⟶[Dop,Set],Sing⁡F(c)(d)=C(Fd, c),\Sing_F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow \big[\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}\big],\qquad \Sing_F(c)(d)=\mathcal{C}\big(Fd,\,c\big),SingF​:C⟶[Dop,Set],SingF​(c)(d)=C(Fd,c),

and let ∣⋅∣F=Lan⁡YF|\cdot|_F=\Lan_{\mathcal{Y}}F∣⋅∣F​=LanY​F, which by Proposition 20.5 exists and is given by the coend

∣X∣F=∫dX(d)⋅Fd.|X|_F=\int^{d}X(d)\cdot Fd .∣X∣F​=∫dX(d)⋅Fd.

Example 20.13 (Geometric realisation). Take D=Δ\mathcal{D}=\DeltaD=Δ, C=Top\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{Top}C=Top, and F([n])=∣Δn∣F([n])=|\Delta^n|F([n])=∣Δn∣ the standard topological simplex. Then

Sing⁡F(Y)n=Top(∣Δn∣,Y)\Sing_F(Y)_n=\mathbf{Top}\big(|\Delta^n|,Y\big)SingF​(Y)n​=Top(∣Δn∣,Y)

is the singular simplicial set of YYY, and the coend

∣X∣F=∫nXn⋅∣Δn∣=(∐nXn×∣Δn∣)/ ⁣∼|X|_F=\int^{n}X_n\cdot|\Delta^n| =\Big(\coprod_n X_n\times|\Delta^n|\Big)\Big/\!\sim∣X∣F​=∫nXn​⋅∣Δn∣=(n∐​Xn​×∣Δn∣)/∼

is the usual geometric realisation: one topological simplex for each simplex of XXX, glued along the face and degeneracy identifications, which is exactly what the coend’s coequaliser imposes.

Example 20.14 (The nerve of a category). Take D=Δ\mathcal{D}=\DeltaD=Δ, C=Cat\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{Cat}C=Cat, and F([n])=[n]F([n])=[n]F([n])=[n] the poset 0→1→⋯→n0\to1\to\cdots\to n0→1→⋯→n viewed as a category. Then Sing⁡F(A)n=Cat([n],A)\Sing_F(\mathcal{A})_n=\mathbf{Cat}([n],\mathcal{A})SingF​(A)n​=Cat([n],A) is the set of strings of nnn composable morphisms: this is the nerve NAN\mathcal{A}NA. The construction ∣⋅∣F|\cdot|_F∣⋅∣F​ is its left adjoint, sending a simplicial set to the category it generates.

Example 20.15 (The pattern in its smallest case). Take D=1\mathcal{D}=\mathbf{1}D=1 and let FFF pick out an object c0∈Cc_0\in\mathcal{C}c0​∈C. Presheaves on 1\mathbf{1}1 are sets, Sing⁡F(c)=C(c0,c)\Sing_F(c)=\mathcal{C}(c_0,c)SingF​(c)=C(c0​,c), and the coend collapses to ∣S∣F=S⋅c0|S|_F=S\cdot c_0∣S∣F​=S⋅c0​, the coproduct of SSS copies of c0c_0c0​. The adjunction to be proved reads

C(∐Sc0, c) ≅ Set(S, C(c0,c)),\mathcal{C}\Big(\coprod_{S}c_0,\ c\Big)\ \cong\ \mathbf{Set}\big(S,\ \mathcal{C}(c_0,c)\big),C(S∐​c0​, c) ≅ Set(S, C(c0​,c)),

which is precisely the universal property of a coproduct: a map out of a coproduct is a family of maps, indexed by SSS. Nothing else is happening in the general case either — the coend is a coproduct glued along D\mathcal{D}D, and the naturality in ddd is what turns the family into a natural transformation.

Remark 20.16 (What remains to be checked). Construction 20.12 produces, for every FFF, a pair of functors; the statement that they are adjoint,

C(∣X∣F, c) ≅ [Dop,Set](X, Sing⁡F(c)),\mathcal{C}\big(|X|_F,\ c\big)\ \cong\ \big[\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}\big]\big(X,\ \Sing_F(c)\big),C(∣X∣F​, c) ≅ [Dop,Set](X, SingF​(c)),

is not yet proved here. Every ingredient is: the Yoneda lemma; the universal property of a left Kan extension (Definition 20.4); the coend formula (Proposition 20.5 and Remark 20.11); and density (Theorem 20.9), which reduces the general XXX to the representables, where the Yoneda lemma applies directly. Example 20.15 shows the shape of the argument in the case where the indexing category contributes nothing.

Proving it is worth doing once, because of what it then yields for free. The special case of Example 20.13 is the adjunction ∣⋅∣⊣Sing⁡|\cdot|\dashv\Sing∣⋅∣⊣Sing between spaces and simplicial sets — the one on which Chapters 22 and 23 depend, and which is usually verified by an explicit and uninformative computation with simplices. Proved at this level, it is a corollary rather than a computation, and the same corollary covers Example 20.14 and every other choice of FFF.

Exercise 20.17 (medium). Grant the adjunction of Remark 20.16. Deduce that geometric realisation preserves all colimits, and that Sing⁡\SingSing preserves all limits.

Solution. ∣⋅∣|\cdot|∣⋅∣ is a left adjoint, so it preserves colimits by Exercise 20.2. Dually Sing⁡\SingSing, being a right adjoint, preserves limits: the same computation with the arrows reversed.

Exercise 20.18 (medium). Let GGG be a group, regarded as a one-object category. Identify ∣NG∣|N G|∣NG∣, the realisation of the nerve, and show it is a K(G,1)K(G,1)K(G,1).

Hint. Describe (NG)n(NG)_n(NG)n​ explicitly, and then identify the realisation with the standard bar construction. For the homotopy type, exhibit a contractible space with a free GGG-action.

Solution. By Example 20.14, (NG)n=Cat([n],G)(NG)_n=\mathbf{Cat}([n],G)(NG)n​=Cat([n],G) is the set of strings of nnn composable arrows, i.e. GnG^nGn, with faces given by multiplying adjacent entries and dropping outer ones. Realising gives the bar construction BGBGBG.

There is a parallel simplicial set EGEGEG with (EG)n=Gn+1(EG)_n=G^{n+1}(EG)n​=Gn+1 and GGG acting freely by diagonal multiplication, whose realisation is contractible — it has an extra degeneracy, so its identity map is homotopic to a constant — and ∣EG∣/G=∣NG∣=BG|EG|/G=|NG|=BG∣EG∣/G=∣NG∣=BG. Hence ∣EG∣→BG|EG|\to BG∣EG∣→BG is a covering with contractible total space, so π1(BG)≅G\pi_1(BG)\cong Gπ1​(BG)≅G by Proposition 3.12 and πn(BG)≅πn(∣EG∣)=0\pi_n(BG)\cong\pi_n(|EG|)=0πn​(BG)≅πn​(∣EG∣)=0 for n≥2n\ge2n≥2 by Exercise 13.7.

20.5 What to carry forward

  • Left adjoints preserve colimits (Exercise 20.2) and nothing else (Warning 20.3).

  • Left Kan extensions (Definition 20.4), their pointwise formula (Proposition 20.5), and absoluteness (Definition 20.7 and Exercise 20.8) — the last is what Chapter 22 needs.

  • Density (Theorem 20.9) and coend notation (Remark 20.11).

  • Construction 20.12: one construction producing geometric realisation, the nerve, and the classifying space (Examples 20.13 and 20.14 and Exercise 20.18); what remains to be verified about it is set out in Remark 20.16.
