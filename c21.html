Strict limits and colimits are not homotopy invariant: replace one of the objects in a diagram by a homotopy equivalent one and the answer can change completely. Since every construction in Parts I–III was only ever defined up to homotopy, this is a serious defect, and the repair — replacing the construction by one that is invariant by design — is the subject of this chapter.

The two model cases are the pushout and the pullback. Their homotopy versions are the double mapping cylinder and the space of paths-with-lifts, and every construction the book has used so far — mapping cone, suspension, homotopy fibre — is one of them.

21.1 Why strict colimits are the wrong ones

Example 21.1 (The same diagram, twice). Consider the two pushout diagrams

∗⟵S1⟶∗,D2⟵S1⟶D2,\ast\longleftarrow S^{1}\longrightarrow\ast, \qquad D^{2}\longleftarrow S^{1}\longrightarrow D^{2},∗⟵S1⟶∗,D2⟵S1⟶D2,

which differ only in that ∗\ast∗ has been replaced by the homotopy equivalent D2D^2D2, and the maps by homotopic ones. The first pushout is a point. The second is S2S^2S2.

So the strict pushout is not an invariant of the homotopy types of the diagram: it distinguishes ∗\ast∗ from D2D^2D2, which no homotopy-theoretic construction may do.

Remark 21.2. The failure is not an accident of the example. A pushout identifies points on the nose, and the identification is sensitive to exactly which points — information that a homotopy destroys. The repair is to glue not along XXX but along a cylinder on XXX, so that the gluing is made only up to a recorded path. That is the definition below, and everything else in the chapter follows the same principle.

21.2 Homotopy pushouts

Definition 21.3. The homotopy pushout of Y←fX→gZY\xleftarrow{f}X\xrightarrow{g}ZYf​Xg​Z is the double mapping cylinder

Y⊔(X×I)⊔Z / ((x,0)∼f(x), (x,1)∼g(x)).Y\sqcup(X\times I)\sqcup Z\ \big/\ \big((x,0)\sim f(x),\ (x,1)\sim g(x)\big).Y⊔(X×I)⊔Z / ((x,0)∼f(x), (x,1)∼g(x)).

Theorem 21.4 (Invariance; recalled). A map of diagrams which is a homotopy equivalence at each of the three objects induces a homotopy equivalence of homotopy pushouts.

Example 21.5 (Suspension). The homotopy pushout of ∗←X→∗\ast\leftarrow X\rightarrow\ast∗←X→∗ is ∗⊔(X×I)⊔∗\ast\sqcup(X\times I)\sqcup\ast∗⊔(X×I)⊔∗ with each end collapsed, which is the unreduced suspension ΣX\Sigma XΣX. Compare Example 21.1: the strict pushout of that diagram was a point, and the homotopy pushout is ΣS1=S2\Sigma S^1=S^2ΣS1=S2 in the case considered there — which is also the strict pushout of the second diagram. The homotopy pushout gives the answer that the well-behaved representative of the diagram gave.

Example 21.6 (Mapping cylinder). The homotopy pushout of Y←fX→ id XY\xleftarrow{f}X\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{id}\ }XYf​X id ​X is the mapping cylinder MfM_fMf​, which deformation retracts onto YYY. So a diagram in which one leg is the identity has homotopy pushout the other object, as it must.

Exercise 21.7 (medium). Show that if f ⁣:X→Yf\colon X\to Yf:X→Y is a cofibration then the natural map from the homotopy pushout of Y←fX→gZY\xleftarrow{f}X\xrightarrow{g}ZYf​Xg​Z to the strict pushout is a homotopy equivalence.

Hint. The double mapping cylinder maps to the strict pushout by collapsing the cylinder coordinate. Use the homotopy extension property to build an inverse up to homotopy.

Solution. Collapsing X×IX\times IX×I onto X×{0}X\times\{0\}X×{0} and applying fff defines a map from the homotopy pushout to Y⊔XZY\sqcup_XZY⊔X​Z. Since fff is a cofibration, so is the inclusion of ZZZ into the double mapping cylinder along X×{1}X\times\{1\}X×{1}, and the collapse X×I→XX\times I\to XX×I→X is a homotopy equivalence rel the ends. The HEP allows that homotopy equivalence of the pieces to be pushed through the gluing: the induced map of pushouts is then a homotopy equivalence, by the gluing lemma for cofibrations.

Remark 21.8 (Which case is left). Two of the three special cases have been done: taking both legs to a point gives the suspension (Example 21.5), and taking one leg to be an identity gives the other object (Example 21.6). The remaining case — one leg a general map fff and the other a constant — is the one that produces the constructions of Chapter 11, and identifying it is a short computation with Definition 21.3: glue a cylinder on XXX to YYY at one end and collapse the other. In the based setting the result is the reduced mapping cone CfCfCf of Exercise 11.6, and working out the identification is the natural exercise here.

21.3 Homotopy pullbacks

Everything above dualises. Where a homotopy pushout glues along a cylinder, recording a path in the source, a homotopy pullback matches up along a path space, recording a path in the target.

Definition 21.9 (The case we shall use). Let f ⁣:X→Yf\colon X\to Yf:X→Y be a map of based spaces. Its homotopy fibre is

Ff={(x,γ)∈X×YI : γ(0)=f(x), γ(1)=y0}.F_f=\big\{(x,\gamma)\in X\times Y^{I}\ :\ \gamma(0)=f(x),\ \gamma(1)=y_0\big\}.Ff​={(x,γ)∈X×YI : γ(0)=f(x), γ(1)=y0​}.

A point of FfF_fFf​ is a point of XXX together with a path witnessing that its image is the basepoint — not a point whose image is the basepoint. That replacement of an equation by a path is the whole idea.

Proposition 21.10. Ff→XF_f\to XFf​→X is a fibration with fibre ΩY\Omega YΩY, and if fff is already a fibration then the inclusion of the strict fibre f−1(y0)↪Fff^{-1}(y_0)\hookrightarrow F_ff−1(y0​)↪Ff​ is a homotopy equivalence.

Example 21.11. Taking X=∗X=\astX=∗, the homotopy fibre of ∗→Y\ast\to Y∗→Y is the space of paths from y0y_0y0​ to y0y_0y0​, that is ΩY\Omega YΩY. Taking fff to be a fibration recovers the long exact sequence of Corollary 14.3, now available for an arbitrary map: every map has a homotopy fibre, and hence a long exact sequence, even when it is not a fibration.

Exercise 21.12 (easy). Give a strict pullback that is not a homotopy pullback, and state a condition under which the two agree.

Solution. Pull back ∗→S1←∗\ast\to S^1\leftarrow\ast∗→S1←∗ where both maps hit the basepoint: the strict pullback is a point. The homotopy version replaces the equation ∗=∗\ast=\ast∗=∗ by a path in S1S^1S1 between the two images, giving ΩS1\Omega S^1ΩS1, which has π0=Z\pi_0=\ZZπ0​=Z and is not a point.

The two agree when one of the maps is a fibration: then the strict pullback is already a homotopy pullback, which is the pullback half of Exercise 21.7 and the reason Proposition 21.10 says what it does.

Remark 21.13 (The general formula). Definition 21.9 is the special case Z=∗Z=\astZ=∗ of a general construction. For a diagram Y→pX←qZY\xrightarrow{p}X\xleftarrow{q}ZYp​Xq​Z, dualising Definition 21.3 means replacing the condition “p(y)p(y)p(y) equals q(z)q(z)q(z)” by “a path in XXX from p(y)p(y)p(y) to q(z)q(z)q(z) is given”, and assembling the triples into a subspace of XI×Y×ZX^I\times Y\times ZXI×Y×Z. Writing down the conditions those triples must satisfy, and checking that the result has the invariance property of Theorem 21.4, is the natural exercise here; the homotopy fibre is the case in which one of the two objects is a point, and it is worth checking that the general formula does specialise to Definition 21.9.

21.4 Exactness

The point of these constructions is that they turn into exact sequences.

Proposition 21.14. Let F→E→pBF\to E\xrightarrow{p}BF→Ep​B be a fibration of based spaces and ZZZ any based space. Then

[Z,F]⟶[Z,E]→ p∗ [Z,B][Z,F]\longrightarrow[Z,E]\xrightarrow{\ p_*\ }[Z,B][Z,F]⟶[Z,E] p∗​ ​[Z,B]

is exact as a sequence of pointed sets: the image of the first map is the preimage of the basepoint under the second.

Proof. A map Z→F→EZ\to F\to EZ→F→E composes to a constant, so the image is contained in the kernel. Conversely, let g ⁣:Z→Eg\colon Z\to Eg:Z→E with p∘gp\circ gp∘g nullhomotopic, and let H ⁣:Z×I→BH\colon Z\times I\to BH:Z×I→B be a nullhomotopy, H0=pgH_0=pgH0​=pg and H1H_1H1​ constant at b0b_0b0​. By the homotopy lifting property, HHH lifts to H~ ⁣:Z×I→E\widetilde H\colon Z\times I\to EH:Z×I→E with H~0=g\widetilde H_0=gH0​=g. Then H~1\widetilde H_1H1​ maps ZZZ into p−1(b0)=Fp^{-1}(b_0)=Fp−1(b0​)=F, and g≃H~1g\simeq\widetilde H_1g≃H1​, so [g][g][g] is in the image.□

Warning 21.15. Exactness here means image === kernel and nothing more. These are pointed sets, not groups, so there is no reason for either map to be injective, and no “exact sequence” reasoning beyond that one identity is available. When ZZZ is a suspension the sets become groups and more can be said, which is what makes the Puppe sequence useful.

Remark 21.16 (The dual statement). Everything in Proposition 21.14 dualises to a cofibration X→fY→CfX\xrightarrow{f}Y\to CfXf​Y→Cf, giving exactness of

[Cf,Z]⟶[Y,Z]⟶[X,Z][Cf,Z]\longrightarrow[Y,Z]\longrightarrow[X,Z][Cf,Z]⟶[Y,Z]⟶[X,Z]

for every ZZZ; the proof replaces the lifting of a nullhomotopy by the extension of one over a cone, and Example 17.4 already observed that a nullhomotopy of a composite is an extension over the cone. Writing out that dual argument is the natural exercise, and combined with Remark 21.8 it turns a cofibration into an exact sequence of pointed sets.

Iterating either version produces the Puppe sequence, in which cones are replaced by suspensions and the pointed sets eventually become groups.

21.5 General homotopy colimits

Definition 21.17. For a diagram D ⁣:J→TopD\colon J\to\mathbf{Top}D:J→Top the homotopy colimit is the realisation of the simplicial replacement

hocolim⁡JD=∣ [n]↦∐j0→⋯→jnD(j0) ∣,\operatorname*{hocolim}_JD =\Big|\ [n]\mapsto\coprod_{j_0\to\cdots\to j_n}D(j_0)\ \Big| ,Jhocolim​D=​ [n]↦j0​→⋯→jn​∐​D(j0​) ​,

the coproduct being over strings of nnn composable morphisms of JJJ. Dually, the homotopy limit is the totalisation of the cosimplicial replacement.

Example 21.18. For JJJ the category ∙←∙→∙\bullet\leftarrow\bullet\rightarrow\bullet∙←∙→∙, unwinding Definition 21.17 in low degrees gives exactly the double mapping cylinder of Definition 21.3: the 000-simplices contribute the three objects and the 111-simplices contribute the two cylinders.

Remark 21.19 (Forward). Definition 21.17 is the definition that Chapter 29 works with. There the question is when a homotopy colimit can be computed over a smaller indexing category — a cofinality statement — and the answer turns a computation into a statement about the contractibility of certain comma categories. Chapter 22 supplies the general framework in which “homotopy colimit” becomes “derived functor of colimit”, which is the conceptual explanation of everything in this chapter.

21.6 What to carry forward

  • Strict colimits are not homotopy invariant (Example 21.1 and Remark 21.2); glue along a cylinder instead (Definition 21.3).

  • Suspension is a homotopy pushout (Example 21.5), and so is the mapping cylinder (Example 21.6).

  • Along a cofibration, strict and homotopy pushouts agree (Exercise 21.7).

  • The homotopy fibre replaces an equation by a path (Definition 21.9), so every map has a long exact sequence (Example 21.11). A strict pullback along a fibration is already a homotopy pullback (Exercise 21.12).

  • Exactness of pointed sets, and only that (Proposition 21.14 and Warning 21.15).

  • Homotopy colimits in general are realisations of simplicial replacements (Definition 21.17).
