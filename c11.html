Chapter 11

Cofibrations, approximation, and connectivity

In this chapter

  1. 11.1 The homotopy extension property
  2. 11.2 Better models
  3. 11.3 The extension lemma
  4. 11.4 What to carry forward

This chapter pays a debt. Twice in Part I we collapsed a contractible subcomplex and asserted that nothing was lost — once to normalise a CW complex to a single 000-cell (Proposition 2.1), and once to read a free basis off a spanning tree (Example 3.6). The justification is the homotopy extension property, and it is proved in Section 11.1.

The rest of the chapter is about replacing a space by a better model of itself. Two theorems do all the work — cellular approximation and the extension lemma — and between them they turn statements about maps into statements about cell dimensions. That translation is the technique of Chapter 17, met here in its simplest form.

11.1 The homotopy extension property

Definition 11.1. A pair (X,A)(X,A)(X,A) has the homotopy extension property (HEP) if every map X×{0}∪A×I→YX\times\{0\}\cup A\times I\to YX×{0}∪A×I→Y extends to X×I→YX\times I\to YX×I→Y. An inclusion A↪XA\hookrightarrow XA↪X with the HEP is a cofibration.

Concretely: a homotopy defined on AAA and a map defined on XXX that agree at time zero can be combined into a homotopy on all of XXX.

Exercise 11.2 (easy). Show that every CW pair has the homotopy extension property.

Hint. It suffices to construct a retraction Dn×I→Dn×{0}∪∂Dn×ID^n\times I\to D^n\times\{0\}\cup\partial D^n\times IDn×I→Dn×{0}∪∂Dn×I, and then to induct over cells.

Solution. For a single cell, project radially from the point (0,2)∈Dn×R(0,2)\in D^n\times\RR(0,2)∈Dn×R: the ray from that point through any (x,t)∈Dn×I(x,t)\in D^n\times I(x,t)∈Dn×I meets Dn×{0}∪∂Dn×ID^n\times\{0\}\cup\partial D^n\times IDn×{0}∪∂Dn×I in exactly one point, and this defines a retraction rrr.

Now let (X,A)(X,A)(X,A) be a CW pair and suppose given FFF on X×{0}∪A×IX\times\{0\}\cup A\times IX×{0}∪A×I. Extend over the cells of X∖AX\setminus AX∖A by induction on dimension: for an nnn-cell with characteristic map Φ ⁣:Dn→X\Phi\colon D^n\to XΦ:Dn→X, the map FFF is already defined on Dn×{0}∪∂Dn×ID^n\times\{0\}\cup\partial D^n\times IDn×{0}∪∂Dn×I after composing with Φ×id\Phi\times\mathrm{id}Φ×id, and precomposing with the retraction rrr extends it over Dn×ID^n\times IDn×I. The extensions agree on overlaps because they are defined cell by cell, and the CW topology makes the resulting function continuous.

Proposition 11.3. Let (X,A)(X,A)(X,A) be a CW pair with AAA contractible. Then the quotient map q ⁣:X→X/Aq\colon X\to X/Aq:X→X/A is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof. Let ht ⁣:A→Ah_t\colon A\to Aht​:A→A be a contraction, h0=idAh_0=\mathrm{id}_Ah0​=idA​ and h1h_1h1​ constant at a0a_0a0​. Example 11.4 (The debt, paid). A spanning tree TTT of a connected graph Γ\GammaΓ is a contractible subcomplex, so Γ→Γ/T\Gamma\to\Gamma/TΓ→Γ/T is a homotopy equivalence and Γ/T\Gamma/TΓ/T is a wedge of circles — one for each edge outside TTT. That is exactly the move made in Example 3.6 and Exercise 3.9, and in the proof of Proposition 3.5.

More generally, a spanning tree of the 111-skeleton of a connected CW complex is a contractible subcomplex, and collapsing it produces a homotopy equivalent complex with a single 000-cell. This is Proposition 2.1.

Warning 11.5. The proof used the HEP exactly once, to promote the contraction of AAA to a homotopy of XXX. Nothing else in the argument would survive its absence: without some hypothesis relating AAA to XXX, the contraction cannot be made to move the rest of the space, and H1H_1H1​ never gets defined. The natural generalisation — replacing “CW pair” by “cofibration” throughout — costs nothing, since Exercise 11.2 was the only place the CW structure was used.

Exercise 11.6 (medium). Let (X,A)(X,A)(X,A) be a CW pair. Show that the mapping cone X∪ACAX\cup_ACAX∪A​CA is homotopy equivalent to X/AX/AX/A.

Hint. CACACA is contractible. Which pair is Proposition 11.3 being applied to?

Solution. Inside X∪ACAX\cup_ACAX∪A​CA the cone CACACA is a contractible subcomplex, so by Proposition 11.3 the quotient map X∪ACA→(X∪ACA)/CAX\cup_ACA\to(X\cup_ACA)/CAX∪A​CA→(X∪A​CA)/CA is a homotopy equivalence. Collapsing the cone identifies all of AAA to a point, so the target is X/AX/AX/A.

11.2 Better models

Theorem 11.7 (Cellular approximation; recalled). Every map between CW complexes is homotopic to a cellular one, and a homotopy between cellular maps may be taken cellular.

Exercise 11.8 (easy). Deduce that the inclusion Xn↪XX^{n}\hookrightarrow XXn↪X of the nnn-skeleton induces an isomorphism on πk\pi_kπk​ for k<nk<nk<n and a surjection for k=nk=nk=n.

Solution. Let k<nk<nk<n. Any map Sk→XS^k\to XSk→X is homotopic to a cellular one by Theorem 11.7, hence lands in Xk⊆XnX^{k}\subseteq X^{n}Xk⊆Xn: this gives surjectivity. If two maps Sk→XnS^k\to X^{n}Sk→Xn become homotopic in XXX, the homotopy Sk×I→XS^k\times I\to XSk×I→X may be taken cellular, so it lands in Xk+1⊆XnX^{k+1}\subseteq X^{n}Xk+1⊆Xn when k+1≤nk+1\le nk+1≤n: this gives injectivity for k<nk<nk<n. For k=nk=nk=n only the surjectivity argument applies.

Theorem 11.9 (Connectivity-adapted models; recalled). Every mmm-connected CW complex is homotopy equivalent to one whose cells occupy only dimension 000 — a single cell there — and dimensions strictly greater than mmm.

Proof sketch. Build a CW approximation from the bottom. Start with a point mapping to the basepoint; having built a complex ZkZ_kZk​ with a map to XXX inducing isomorphisms on πi\pi_iπi​ for i<ki<ki<k and a surjection on πk\pi_kπk​, attach cells of dimension k+1k+1k+1 to kill the kernel on πk\pi_kπk​ and cells of dimension k+1k+1k+1 to hit new generators of πk+1\pi_{k+1}πk+1​. When XXX is mmm-connected the first mmm stages require no cells at all, because there is nothing to hit and nothing to kill; the construction therefore produces cells only in dimension 000 and in dimensions >m>m>m.□

Example 11.10. SnS^nSn is (n−1)(n-1)(n−1)-connected, and the standard CW structure — one 000-cell and one nnn-cell — is already of the shape promised by Theorem 11.9. A simply connected complex may always be modelled with no 111-cells, which is why arguments about simply connected spaces so often begin “we may assume there are no cells in dimension one”.

11.3 The extension lemma

Everything above is preparation for a single statement, which converts a question about extending maps into a question about the dimensions of cells.

Theorem 11.11 (Extension lemma). Let (X,A)(X,A)(X,A) be a CW pair and YYY a space with πk−1(Y)=0\pi_{k-1}(Y)=0πk−1​(Y)=0 for every kkk such that X∖AX\setminus AX∖A contains a cell of dimension kkk. Then every map A→YA\to YA→Y extends to XXX.

Proof sketch. Extend over cells by induction on dimension. To extend over a kkk-cell with attaching map φ ⁣:Sk−1→Xk−1\varphi\colon S^{k-1}\to X^{k-1}φ:Sk−1→Xk−1, one must show the composite Sk−1→Xk−1→YS^{k-1}\to X^{k-1}\to YSk−1→Xk−1→Y is nullhomotopic; its class lies in πk−1(Y)\pi_{k-1}(Y)πk−1​(Y), which vanishes by hypothesis, and a nullhomotopy is precisely an extension over the cell.□

Remark 11.12. That proof is obstruction theory in embryo, and it is worth naming the shape now: an extension is built skeleton by skeleton; at each stage the obstruction to continuing is the homotopy class of a composite; and if the relevant homotopy group vanishes, the obstruction vanishes with it. Chapter 17 keeps track of the obstructions when they do not vanish, and organises them into cohomology classes with coefficients in πk−1(Y)\pi_{k-1}(Y)πk−1​(Y). The proof of Proposition 2.15 in Chapter 2 was the same argument, run against Y=S1Y=S^1Y=S1 with πk(S1)=0\pi_{k}(S^1)=0πk​(S1)=0 for k≥2k\ge2k≥2.

Exercise 11.13 (medium). Let YYY be nnn-connected and let XXX be a CW complex of dimension at most nnn. Show that every map X→YX\to YX→Y is nullhomotopic.

Hint. A nullhomotopy of f ⁣:X→Yf\colon X\to Yf:X→Y is an extension over X×IX\times IX×I of the map defined on X×{0,1}X\times\{0,1\}X×{0,1} by fff and by a constant. What are the cells of X×IX\times IX×I relative to X×{0,1}X\times\{0,1\}X×{0,1}, and what dimensions do they have?

Solution. Put W=X×IW=X\times IW=X×I and A=X×{0,1}A=X\times\{0,1\}A=X×{0,1}, a CW pair. Define ggg on AAA by fff on X×{0}X\times\{0\}X×{0} and by the constant map on X×{1}X\times\{1\}X×{1}. A cell of W∖AW\setminus AW∖A is e×(0,1)e\times(0,1)e×(0,1) for a cell eee of XXX, so its dimension is dim⁡e+1≤n+1\dim e+1\le n+1dime+1≤n+1. The extension lemma therefore requires πk−1(Y)=0\pi_{k-1}(Y)=0πk−1​(Y)=0 for k≤n+1k\le n+1k≤n+1, i.e. πj(Y)=0\pi_j(Y)=0πj​(Y)=0 for j≤nj\le nj≤n, which is exactly nnn-connectedness. So ggg extends over X×IX\times IX×I, and the extension is a homotopy from fff to a constant map.

Remark 11.14 (Where connectivity arguments go next). Two directions open here, and both are used repeatedly later.

The first is to feed a space into Exercise 11.13 as both source and target, or as source and target after replacing one of them by a connectivity-adapted model from Theorem 11.9. Such arguments turn a numerical coincidence between connectivity and dimension into a statement about the space itself, and they need nothing beyond this page.

The second is to estimate the connectivity of a pair by counting the dimensions of its relative cells. Theorem 11.9 is what makes this work: after replacing XXX and YYY by good models, the relative cells of a product are products of cells, and their dimensions add. That is the standard route to connectivity estimates for products and smash products, and the elementary shadow of the Blakers–Massey theorem.

11.4 What to carry forward

  • CW pairs have the HEP (Exercise 11.2); collapsing a contractible subcomplex is a homotopy equivalence (Proposition 11.3), which is the promise made in Chapters 2 and 3.

  • The HEP is used exactly once in that proof, to move a homotopy of AAA out into XXX (Warning 11.5).

  • Mapping cone ≃\simeq≃ quotient for a CW pair (Exercise 11.6).

  • Cellular approximation makes homotopy groups skeletal (Exercise 11.8); connectivity can be built into the model (Theorem 11.9).

  • The extension lemma (Theorem 11.11) trades homotopy groups against cell dimensions; it is obstruction theory with all obstructions assumed to vanish (Remark 11.12).
