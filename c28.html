Chapter 28

Spectra: why symmetric, and where the signs come from

In this chapter

  1. 28.1 Spectra
  2. 28.2 Where the signs come from
  3. 28.3 Moore spectra
  4. 28.4 What to carry forward

Stable homotopy theory studies what survives suspension. Theorem 14.12 showed that homotopy groups of spheres stabilise; a spectrum is the object whose homotopy groups are the stable ones by construction, and the stable homotopy category is where such objects live.

The technical difficulty, and the reason this chapter exists, is the smash product. Sequential spectra carry one only up to homotopy and with no coherent symmetry, which is fatal for any discussion of ring objects. Symmetric spectra repair this by adding group actions, and the actions are not bookkeeping: forget them and the theory collapses in two different ways.

28.1 Spectra

Definition 28.1. A spectrum XXX is a sequence of based spaces XnX_nXn​ together with structure maps σn ⁣:ΣXn→Xn+1\sigma_n\colon\Sigma X_n\to X_{n+1}σn​:ΣXn​→Xn+1​. Its naive homotopy groups are

π^k(X)=colim⁡n πk+n(Xn),\hat\pi_k(X)=\operatorname*{colim}_n\ \pi_{k+n}(X_n),π^k​(X)=ncolim​ πk+n​(Xn​),

the colimit being taken over suspension followed by σn\sigma_nσn​.

Example 28.2. The sphere spectrum S\mathbb{S}S has Sn=Sn\mathbb{S}_n=S^nSn​=Sn with the identity structure maps. Its naive homotopy groups are π^k(S)=colim⁡nπk+n(Sn)\hat\pi_k(\mathbb{S})=\operatorname*{colim}_n\pi_{k+n}(S^n)π^k​(S)=colimn​πk+n​(Sn). By Theorem 14.12 this colimit stabilises, and its value is the stable stem πks\pi^s_kπks​ of Example 14.13.

A based space YYY gives a suspension spectrum Σ∞Y\Sigma^\infty YΣ∞Y, with (Σ∞Y)n=ΣnY(\Sigma^\infty Y)_n=\Sigma^nY(Σ∞Y)n​=ΣnY; its naive homotopy is the stable homotopy of YYY. The Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum HπH\piHπ has (Hπ)n=K(π,n)(H\pi)_n=K(\pi,n)(Hπ)n​=K(π,n), its structure maps being adjoint to the equivalences K(π,n)≃ΩK(π,n+1)K(\pi,n)\simeq\Omega K(\pi,n+1)K(π,n)≃ΩK(π,n+1) of Exercise 17.15.

Definition 28.3. A symmetric spectrum is a spectrum in which each XnX_nXn​ carries an action of Σn\Sigma_nΣn​, such that the iterated structure map

σm ⁣:Xn∧Sm⟶Xn+m\sigma^m\colon X_n\wedge S^m\longrightarrow X_{n+m}σm:Xn​∧Sm⟶Xn+m​

is Σn×Σm\Sigma_n\times\Sigma_mΣn​×Σm​-equivariant. Here Σm\Sigma_mΣm​ permutes the coordinates of Sm=(Rm)+S^m=(\RR^m)^+Sm=(Rm)+, and acts on Xn+mX_{n+m}Xn+m​ via the inclusion Σm⊆Σn+m\Sigma_m\subseteq\Sigma_{n+m}Σm​⊆Σn+m​ onto the last mmm letters.

Example 28.4. The sphere spectrum is symmetric: Σn\Sigma_nΣn​ acts on Sn=(Rn)+S^n=(\RR^n)^+Sn=(Rn)+ by permuting coordinates, and the structure maps are equivariant because smashing SnS^nSn with SmS^mSm concatenates coordinates.

Warning 28.5 (The actions carry information). It is tempting to regard the symmetric group actions of Definition 28.3 as bookkeeping introduced to make the smash product associative. They are not. Two independent defects appear when the naive homotopy groups of Definition 28.1 are used in place of the correct stable homotopy groups, and both are visible on symmetric spectra:

  • a symmetric spectrum whose group actions are trivial in infinitely many levels has no naive homotopy at all, so forgetting the actions destroys everything;

  • naive homotopy groups fail to commute with infinite products, unlike the genuine ones.

Both are worth establishing for oneself, and the tools for each are supplied by Exercises 28.6 and 28.7 below. Neither is a pathology invented for the purpose: the first is what forces the equivariance in Definition 28.3 to be taken seriously, and the second is why the stable homotopy category cannot be built from π^∗\hat\pi_*π^∗​ alone.

Exercise 28.6 (easy). Let A0→A1→⋯A_0\to A_1\to\cdotsA0​→A1​→⋯ be a sequence of abelian groups in which, for cofinally many nnn, the map An→An+1A_n\to A_{n+1}An​→An+1​ is zero. Show colim⁡nAn=0\operatorname*{colim}_nA_n=0colimn​An​=0.

Solution. An element of the colimit is represented by some a∈Ana\in A_na∈An​, two representatives being identified when they agree after mapping far enough along. Choose m>nm>nm>n with Am→Am+1A_m\to A_{m+1}Am​→Am+1​ zero; the image of aaa in Am+1A_{m+1}Am+1​ is then 000. So every element of the colimit is zero.

Exercise 28.7 (easy). Let Zn↪Zn+1\ZZ^n\hookrightarrow\ZZ^{n+1}Zn↪Zn+1 be the inclusion of the first nnn coordinates. Show colim⁡nZn=⨁n≥1Z\operatorname*{colim}_n\ZZ^n=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\ZZcolimn​Zn=⨁n≥1​Z, and contrast this with ∏n≥1Z\prod_{n\ge1}\ZZ∏n≥1​Z.

Solution. Every element of the colimit comes from some Zn\ZZ^nZn, hence has only finitely many nonzero coordinates; conversely every finitely supported sequence appears. So the colimit is ⨁Z\bigoplus\ZZ⨁Z, which is countable, while ∏Z\prod\ZZ∏Z is uncountable — the same distinction as Example 5.3.

28.2 Where the signs come from

Graded commutativity has been in force since Chapter 7, and the Koszul sign rule has been used in Chapter 25 without comment. Both come from one computation.

Lemma 28.8. Let τ∈Σk\tau\in\Sigma_kτ∈Σk​ act on Sk=(Rk)+S^k=(\RR^k)^+Sk=(Rk)+ by permuting coordinates. Then deg⁡τ=sgn(τ)\deg\tau=\mathrm{sgn}(\tau)degτ=sgn(τ). In particular the shuffle exchanging a block of nnn coordinates with a block of mmm coordinates has degree (−1)nm(-1)^{nm}(−1)nm.

Proof. Permuting coordinates is the restriction to Rk\RR^kRk of a linear automorphism whose matrix is the permutation matrix of τ\tauτ, with determinant sgn(τ)\mathrm{sgn}(\tau)sgn(τ). A linear automorphism of Rk\RR^kRk extends to the one-point compactification SkS^kSk, and its degree is the sign of its determinant: through linear automorphisms it is homotopic to the identity when the determinant is positive and to a single reflection when it is negative.

For the block shuffle, moving each of the nnn coordinates of the first block past each of the mmm coordinates of the second requires nmnmnm transpositions, so its sign is (−1)nm(-1)^{nm}(−1)nm.□

Remark 28.9 (The sign rule is a theorem). Lemma 28.8 is the source of every Koszul sign in the book. In the stable category an element of degree nnn is represented by a map out of a sphere SnS^nSn, and interchanging two such elements requires interchanging the corresponding sphere coordinates; the resulting map differs from the original by the shuffle, hence by the factor (−1)nm(-1)^{nm}(−1)nm.

Making that precise means constructing a symmetric monoidal structure on a category of formal desuspensions — objects (X,n)(X,n)(X,n) with morphisms the stable maps, smash defined by (X,n)∧(Y,m)=(X∧Y,n+m)(X,n)\wedge(Y,m)=(X\wedge Y,n+m)(X,n)∧(Y,m)=(X∧Y,n+m) — and checking the coherence axioms, with Lemma 28.8 supplying the symmetry isomorphism. That construction is the natural exercise here, and its value is that afterwards the sign rule is never again a convention to be remembered.

Note also where the difficulty in Definition 28.3 comes from. The symmetry has to be present at the level of spaces, coherently and for all permutations at once, which is precisely what the Σn\Sigma_nΣn​-actions provide and what a merely sequential spectrum lacks.

28.3 Moore spectra

Definition 28.10. For an integer mmm, the Moore spectrum S/m\mathbb{S}/mS/m is the cofibre of m ⁣:S→Sm\colon\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{S}m:S→S.

Exercise 28.11 (easy). Show that H∗(S/2;F2)H^*(\mathbb{S}/2;\FF_2)H∗(S/2;F2​) is F2\FF_2F2​ in degrees 000 and 111, with Sq⁡1\Sq^1Sq1 carrying the degree-zero class to the degree-one class.

Hint. Use the long exact sequence of the cofibre sequence S→2S→S/2\mathbb{S}\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{S}/2S2​S→S/2 in mod-222 cohomology, and recall from Chapter 9 which operation the coefficient sequence 0→Z→2Z→F2→00\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{2}\ZZ\to\FF_2\to00→Z2​Z→F2​→0 produces.

Solution. Mod 222, the map 2 ⁣:S→S2\colon\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{S}2:S→S induces zero on cohomology, so the long exact sequence of the cofibre sequence breaks into short exact pieces and gives H∗(S/2;F2)=F2H^*(\mathbb{S}/2;\FF_2)=\FF_2H∗(S/2;F2​)=F2​ in degrees 000 and 111 and zero elsewhere.

The connecting map of that cofibre sequence is, by construction, the operation associated with reduction modulo 222 — the Bockstein of Definition 9.11. So it carries the degree-zero generator to the degree-one generator, and a degree-one operation acts nontrivially between them.

Exercise 28.12 (easy). Show that for a wedge of spectra, H∗(X∨Y;F2)≅H∗(X;F2)⊕H∗(Y;F2)H^*(X\vee Y;\FF_2)\cong H^*(X;\FF_2)\oplus H^*(Y;\FF_2)H∗(X∨Y;F2​)≅H∗(X;F2​)⊕H∗(Y;F2​) as modules over the Steenrod algebra.

Solution. The inclusions and retractions of a wedge induce, exactly as in Exercise 7.3, a splitting of H∗(X∨Y)H^*(X\vee Y)H∗(X∨Y) as a direct sum. Both maps are induced by maps of spectra, and Steenrod operations are natural, so the splitting is one of A\mathcal{A}A-modules: an operation applied to a class from one summand stays in that summand.

Remark 28.13 (An algebraic structure that does not exist). Put the two exercises together and a question presents itself. The smash square S/2∧S/2\mathbb{S}/2\wedge\mathbb{S}/2S/2∧S/2 has mod-222 cohomology in degrees 0,1,1,20,1,1,20,1,1,2, by Künneth. If it were equivalent to a wedge S/2∨ΣS/2\mathbb{S}/2\vee\Sigma\mathbb{S}/2S/2∨ΣS/2 then by Exercise 28.12 its cohomology would split as an A\mathcal{A}A-module, with the degree-zero class and the degree-two class in different summands — so every operation carrying one to the other would have to vanish. In particular Sq⁡2\Sq^2Sq2 would vanish on the bottom class.

Whether it does is a computation, and it decides the question. A negative answer also settles a second one: a homotopy ring structure on S/2\mathbb{S}/2S/2 would force the splitting, so its non-existence would follow. Carrying this out is the natural exercise, and it uses only Exercise 28.11, Exercise 28.12 and the Cartan formula.

The moral is worth stating in advance. The mod-222 Moore spectrum is the standing counterexample of stable homotopy theory: the cofibre of a self-map of the sphere, as simple an object as one can construct, and it is not a ring. Any theory that assumes such cofibres are well behaved is wrong.

28.4 What to carry forward

  • Spectra and naive homotopy groups (Definition 28.1 and Example 28.2); symmetric spectra add equivariance (Definition 28.3).

  • The actions are not bookkeeping (Warning 28.5); the two colimit lemmas (Exercises 28.6 and 28.7) are what establish that.

  • The Koszul sign is the degree of a permutation (Lemma 28.8 and Remark 28.9), not a convention.

  • H∗(S/2;F2)H^*(\mathbb{S}/2;\FF_2)H∗(S/2;F2​) and the wedge splitting (Exercises 28.11 and 28.12) detect the failure of S/2\mathbb{S}/2S/2 to be a ring (Remark 28.13).
