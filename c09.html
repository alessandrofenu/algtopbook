Chapter 9

Towers, inverse limits, and Bocksteins

In this chapter

  1. 9.1 The derived functor of the inverse limit, as a cokernel
  2. 9.2 The Bockstein as a differential
  3. 9.3 What to carry forward

Two constructions in this chapter look unrelated and are not. Both concern a sequence of approximations to an object, and both measure the failure of an obvious guess.

The inverse limit of a tower of groups is not right exact, and its first derived functor lim←⁡1\varprojlim^1lim​1 is the error term. It appears whenever the cohomology of an infinite complex is assembled from its skeleta, and it is usually zero — but only for a reason, and the reason is worth knowing.

The Bockstein is the error term of a different reduction: H∗(X;Fp)H^*(X;\FF_p)H∗(X;Fp​) does not determine H∗(X;Z)H^*(X;\ZZ)H∗(X;Z), and β\betaβ is the first approximation to the difference. It is a differential on mod-ppp cohomology whose homology detects which torsion has order exactly ppp. Chapter 18 will identify β\betaβ with the first Steenrod square, and Chapter 26 will use that identification to write an integral characteristic class as a Bockstein.

9.1 The derived functor of the inverse limit, as a cokernel

Let (Gi,αi)(G_i,\alpha_i)(Gi​,αi​) be a tower of abelian groups,

⋯⟶G2→ α2 G1→ α1 G0.\cdots\longrightarrow G_2\xrightarrow{\ \alpha_2\ }G_1 \xrightarrow{\ \alpha_1\ }G_0 .⋯⟶G2​ α2​ ​G1​ α1​ ​G0​.

Definition 9.1. Let Δ ⁣:∏iGi→∏iGi\Delta\colon\prod_iG_i\to\prod_iG_iΔ:∏i​Gi​→∏i​Gi​ be given by Δ(x)i=xi−αi+1(xi+1)\Delta(x)_i=x_i-\alpha_{i+1}(x_{i+1})Δ(x)i​=xi​−αi+1​(xi+1​). Then lim←⁡Gi=ker⁡Δ\varprojlim G_i=\ker\Deltalim​Gi​=kerΔ and lim←⁡1Gi=coker⁡Δ\varprojlim^1G_i=\coker\Deltalim​1Gi​=cokerΔ.

So lim←⁡1\varprojlim^1lim​1 measures the failure of the system of equations xi−αi+1(xi+1)=yix_i-\alpha_{i+1}(x_{i+1})=y_ixi​−αi+1​(xi+1​)=yi​ to be solvable. That is the only description we need: every computation below either solves those equations or shows they cannot be solved.

Exercise 9.2 (easy). Show that lim←⁡1Gi=0\varprojlim^1G_i=0lim​1Gi​=0 if every αi\alpha_iαi​ is surjective.

Hint. Solve the equations recursively, choosing a preimage at each stage.

Solution. Given y∈∏Giy\in\prod G_iy∈∏Gi​, set x0=0x_0=0x0​=0 and choose inductively xi+1x_{i+1}xi+1​ with αi+1(xi+1)=xi−yi\alpha_{i+1}(x_{i+1})=x_i-y_iαi+1​(xi+1​)=xi​−yi​, which is possible because αi+1\alpha_{i+1}αi+1​ is onto. Then Δ(x)=y\Delta(x)=yΔ(x)=y, so Δ\DeltaΔ is surjective and its cokernel vanishes.

Exercise 9.3 (medium). Call a tower pro-zero if for every iii some composite Gi+n→GiG_{i+n}\to G_iGi+n​→Gi​ is zero. Show that a pro-zero tower has lim←⁡Gi=0\varprojlim G_i=0lim​Gi​=0 and lim←⁡1Gi=0\varprojlim^1G_i=0lim​1Gi​=0.

Solution. The sum is finite, since α(n)\alpha^{(n)}α(n) into GiG_iGi​ vanishes for large nnn. With that definition,

αi+1(xi+1)=∑n≥0α(n+1)(yi+1+n)=∑m≥1α(m)(yi+m),\alpha_{i+1}(x_{i+1})=\sum_{n\ge0}\alpha^{(n+1)}(y_{i+1+n}) =\sum_{m\ge1}\alpha^{(m)}(y_{i+m}),αi+1​(xi+1​)=n≥0∑​α(n+1)(yi+1+n​)=m≥1∑​α(m)(yi+m​),

so xi−αi+1(xi+1)=α(0)(yi)=yix_i-\alpha_{i+1}(x_{i+1})=\alpha^{(0)}(y_i)=y_ixi​−αi+1​(xi+1​)=α(0)(yi​)=yi​, and Δ\DeltaΔ is onto. For the limit, a compatible family satisfies xi=α(n)(xi+n)=0x_i=\alpha^{(n)}(x_{i+n})=0xi​=α(n)(xi+n​)=0 once nnn is large.

Theorem 9.4 (Six-term sequence; recalled). A short exact sequence of towers 0→A→B→C→00\to A\to B\to C\to00→A→B→C→0 induces an exact sequence

0→lim←⁡A→lim←⁡B→lim←⁡C→lim←⁡1A→lim←⁡1B→lim←⁡1C→0.0\to\varprojlim A\to\varprojlim B\to\varprojlim C \to\varprojlim{}^1A\to\varprojlim{}^1B\to\varprojlim{}^1C\to0 .0→lim​A→lim​B→lim​C→lim​1A→lim​1B→lim​1C→0.

Definition 9.5. Write Gi(n)=im⁡(Gi+n→Gi)G_i^{(n)}=\im\big(G_{i+n}\to G_i\big)Gi(n)​=im(Gi+n​→Gi​), a descending chain of subgroups of GiG_iGi​. The tower satisfies the Mittag-Leffler condition if for each iii that chain is eventually constant, with stable value Gi∞G_i^{\infty}Gi∞​.

Warning 9.6. The condition does not say that the maps are eventually surjective. It says that inside each fixed GiG_iGi​ the images of the groups far above stop shrinking. A tower whose maps are eventually zero satisfies it, with Gi∞=0G_i^{\infty}=0Gi∞​=0, and no map in it need be surjective.

Remark 9.7 (The step that remains). The subgroups Gi∞G_i^{\infty}Gi∞​ form a subtower whose transition maps are surjective, and under Mittag-Leffler the quotient tower Gi/Gi∞G_i/G_i^{\infty}Gi​/Gi∞​ is pro-zero: for large nnn the image of Gi+nG_{i+n}Gi+n​ in GiG_iGi​ is already Gi∞G_i^{\infty}Gi∞​, so the composite into Gi/Gi∞G_i/G_i^{\infty}Gi​/Gi∞​ vanishes. Feeding that short exact sequence of towers into Theorem 9.4, and applying Exercises 9.2 and 9.3 to the two outer terms, yields the standard vanishing criterion in a line. Assembling it is the natural exercise to set oneself here; the only thing to watch is Warning 9.6.

Exercise 9.2 might suggest that lim←⁡1\varprojlim^1lim​1 is a formality.

Example 9.8 (A lim←⁡1\varprojlim^1lim​1 that is uncountable). Fix a prime ppp, let Gi=ZG_i=\ZZGi​=Z for all iii, and let every transition map be multiplication by ppp. Then lim←⁡Gi=0\varprojlim G_i=0lim​Gi​=0, since a compatible family has x0x_0x0​ divisible by pnp^npn for every nnn.

For the derived functor, consider Σ ⁣:∏iZ→Zp\Sigma\colon\prod_i\ZZ\to\ZZ_pΣ:∏i​Z→Zp​ into the ppp-adic integers, Σ(y)=∑ipiyi\Sigma(y)=\sum_ip^iy_iΣ(y)=∑i​piyi​. The series converges ppp-adically, and Σ\SigmaΣ is surjective because every ppp-adic integer has such an expansion. Composing with Δ\DeltaΔ telescopes:

Σ(Δ(x))=∑ipi(xi−pxi+1)=lim⁡N→∞(x0−pNxN)=x0,\Sigma\big(\Delta(x)\big)=\sum_ip^i\big(x_i-px_{i+1}\big) =\lim_{N\to\infty}\big(x_0-p^{N}x_{N}\big)=x_0 ,Σ(Δ(x))=i∑​pi(xi​−pxi+1​)=N→∞lim​(x0​−pNxN​)=x0​,

because pNxN→0p^Nx_N\to0pNxN​→0 in Zp\ZZ_pZp​. So Σ\SigmaΣ maps im⁡Δ\im\DeltaimΔ onto Z⊆Zp\ZZ\subseteq\ZZ_pZ⊆Zp​, and it descends to an isomorphism

lim←⁡1 Z  ≅  Zp/Z,\varprojlim{}^1\,\ZZ\;\cong\;\ZZ_p/\ZZ ,lim​1Z≅Zp​/Z,

an uncountable group. A tower of copies of Z\ZZZ with the simplest possible maps already has an enormous lim←⁡1\varprojlim^1lim​1.

Theorem 9.9 (Milnor sequence; recalled). Let the CW complex XXX be the union of an increasing sequence of subcomplexes X0⊆X1⊆⋯X_0\subseteq X_1\subseteq\cdotsX0​⊆X1​⊆⋯. For every nnn there is a short exact sequence

Solution. Each restriction Hk(CPn+1;Z)→Hk(CPn;Z)H^k(\CP^{n+1};\ZZ)\to H^k(\CP^{n};\ZZ)Hk(CPn+1;Z)→Hk(CPn;Z) is surjective: for kkk even and k≤2nk\le2nk≤2n both groups are Z\ZZZ and the generator restricts to the generator, and otherwise the target is 000. So the tower of cohomology groups has surjective transition maps and lim←⁡1=0\varprojlim^1=0lim​1=0 by Exercise 9.2. Hence Hk(CP∞;Z)≅lim←⁡nHk(CPn;Z)H^k(\CP^\infty;\ZZ)\cong \varprojlim_nH^k(\CP^n;\ZZ)Hk(CP∞;Z)≅lim​n​Hk(CPn;Z), which is Z\ZZZ for kkk even and 000 for kkk odd.

9.2 The Bockstein as a differential

Definition 9.11. The coefficient sequence 0→Z/p→Z/p2→Z/p→00\to\ZZ/p\to\ZZ/p^2\to\ZZ/p\to00→Z/p→Z/p2→Z/p→0 induces a natural connecting homomorphism

β ⁣:Hn(X;Fp)⟶Hn+1(X;Fp),\beta\colon H^n(X;\FF_p)\longrightarrow H^{n+1}(X;\FF_p),β:Hn(X;Fp​)⟶Hn+1(X;Fp​),

the Bockstein. The sequence 0→Z→pZ→Fp→00\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{p}\ZZ\to\FF_p\to00→Zp​Z→Fp​→0 induces the integral Bockstein β~ ⁣:Hn(X;Fp)→Hn+1(X;Z)\tilde\beta\colon H^n(X;\FF_p)\to H^{n+1}(X;\ZZ)β~​:Hn(X;Fp​)→Hn+1(X;Z), of which β\betaβ is the mod-ppp reduction.

Exercise 9.12 (medium). Show that β2=0\beta^2=0β2=0 and that β\betaβ is a derivation: β(xy)=β(x) y+(−1)∣x∣x β(y)\beta(xy)=\beta(x)\,y+(-1)^{|x|}x\,\beta(y)β(xy)=β(x)y+(−1)∣x∣xβ(y).

Hint. For the first, the composite Z/p↪Z/p2↠Z/p\ZZ/p\hookrightarrow\ZZ/p^2\twoheadrightarrow\ZZ/pZ/p↪Z/p2↠Z/p occurring in two consecutive connecting maps is zero. For the second, lift cocycles to Z/p2\ZZ/p^2Z/p2-cochains and differentiate the product of the lifts.

Solution. Applying the connecting map twice factors through Z/p↪Z/p2↠Z/p\ZZ/p\hookrightarrow\ZZ/p^2\twoheadrightarrow\ZZ/pZ/p↪Z/p2↠Z/p, which is zero; hence β2=0\beta^2=0β2=0.

The product a~b~\tilde a\tilde ba~b~ lifts ababab, and

δ(a~b~)=(δa~) b~+(−1)∣a∣ a~ (δb~)=p(a′b~+(−1)∣a∣a~ b′).\delta(\tilde a\tilde b)=(\delta\tilde a)\,\tilde b +(-1)^{|a|}\,\tilde a\,(\delta\tilde b) =p\big(a'\tilde b+(-1)^{|a|}\tilde a\,b'\big).δ(a~b~)=(δa~)b~+(−1)∣a∣a~(δb~)=p(a′b~+(−1)∣a∣a~b′).

Reducing mod ppp gives the stated formula.

Since β2=0\beta^2=0β2=0, one may take homology with respect to it.

Definition 9.13. The Bockstein homology of XXX is H(H∗(X;Fp),β)H\big(H^*(X;\FF_p),\beta\big)H(H∗(X;Fp​),β).

Proposition 9.14 (What β\betaβ detects; recalled). If H∗(X;Z)H^*(X;\ZZ)H∗(X;Z) is finitely generated in each degree, the Bockstein homology of XXX is isomorphic to (H∗(X;Z)/torsion)⊗Fp\big(H^*(X;\ZZ)/\text{torsion}\big)\otimes\FF_p(H∗(X;Z)/torsion)⊗Fp​. It therefore vanishes in positive degrees exactly when the free part of H∗(X;Z)H^*(X;\ZZ)H∗(X;Z) sits in degree 000 and every ppp-torsion class has order exactly ppp.

Example 9.15 (β\betaβ on a Moore space). For X=M(Z/p,n)X=M(\ZZ/p,n)X=M(Z/p,n) the reduced mod-ppp cohomology is Fp\FF_pFp​ in degrees nnn and n+1n+1n+1, and β ⁣:Hn→Hn+1\beta\colon H^n\to H^{n+1}β:Hn→Hn+1 is an isomorphism: by Exercise 6.6 the integral cohomology is Z/p\ZZ/pZ/p in degree n+1n+1n+1, on which multiplication by ppp is zero, so the connecting map of 0→Z→pZ→Fp→00\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{p}\ZZ\to\FF_p\to00→Zp​Z→Fp​→0 is nonzero.

For X=M(Z/p2,n)X=M(\ZZ/p^2,n)X=M(Z/p2,n) the mod-ppp cohomology consists of exactly the same two groups, but now β=0\beta=0β=0: the integral torsion has order p2p^2p2, and multiplication by ppp on Z/p2\ZZ/p^2Z/p2 is not injective, which kills the connecting map.

Two spaces with identical mod-ppp cohomology groups are thus separated by β\betaβ. It sees the order of the torsion, not merely its presence.

Example 9.16 (Infinite projective space). H∗(RP∞;F2)=F2[x]H^*(\RP^\infty;\FF_2)=\FF_2[x]H∗(RP∞;F2​)=F2​[x] with ∣x∣=1|x|=1∣x∣=1, and βx=x2\beta x=x^2βx=x2 — Example 9.15 in degree one, RP2=M(Z/2,1)\RP^2=M(\ZZ/2,1)RP2=M(Z/2,1) being the 222-skeleton. By Exercise 9.12,

β(xk)=k xk+1,\beta(x^k)=k\,x^{k+1},β(xk)=kxk+1,

which is xk+1x^{k+1}xk+1 for kkk odd and 000 for kkk even. So ker⁡β\ker\betakerβ has basis 1,x2,x4,…1,x^2,x^4,\dots1,x2,x4,… and im⁡β\im\betaimβ has basis x2,x4,…x^2,x^4,\dotsx2,x4,…, and the Bockstein homology is F2\FF_2F2​ in degree 000 and zero in every positive degree.

Read through Proposition 9.14, that says the free part of H∗(RP∞;Z)H^*(\RP^\infty;\ZZ)H∗(RP∞;Z) lives in degree 000 and every torsion class has order exactly 222 — both true by Example 6.2.

Remark 9.18 (Three continuations). First, β\betaβ is only the first approximation. Torsion of order prp^rpr is detected by an rrr-th Bockstein defined on the previous Bockstein homology, and the family assembles into the Bockstein spectral sequence, whose E∞E_\inftyE∞​-page is the free part mod ppp and whose rrr-th differential counts torsion of order prp^rpr. Example 9.15 is the case r=1r=1r=1 against r=2r=2r=2.

Second, because β\betaβ is a derivation squaring to zero, the mod-ppp cohomology of a product is a tensor product of complexes and its Bockstein homology is the tensor product of the Bockstein homologies. Applied to a smash power of a Moore space, and read back through Proposition 9.14, that determines the integral cohomology of the smash power completely. It is the natural consolidating computation for this chapter, and everything it needs is now available.

Third, β\betaβ is not merely a connecting homomorphism: it is a stable cohomology operation, natural in XXX and commuting with suspension. Chapter 18 identifies it with Sq⁡1\Sq^1Sq1, and that identification is what lets Chapter 26 express an integral Euler class as a Bockstein.

9.3 What to carry forward

  • lim←⁡1\varprojlim^1lim​1 is the cokernel of 1−shift1-\text{shift}1−shift (Definition 9.1). It vanishes for surjective towers (Exercise 9.2) and for pro-zero ones (Exercise 9.3); those two facts and Theorem 9.4 give the general criterion (Remark 9.7).

  • Mittag-Leffler is about images stabilising, not about surjectivity (Warning 9.6).

  • lim←⁡1\varprojlim^1lim​1 can be uncountable (Example 9.8), and it is the error term in the Milnor sequence (Theorem 9.9).

  • β\betaβ is a derivation with β2=0\beta^2=0β2=0 (Exercise 9.12), so mod-ppp cohomology is a complex, and its homology detects the free part together with the order of the torsion (Proposition 9.14, Examples 9.15 and 9.16, and Exercise 9.17).
