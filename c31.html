Lie models, the rational Hurewicz map, and A∞A_\inftyA∞​-structures

The last chapter of the book collects three things that have been converging since Part III: the algebraic model of a rational homotopy type, the map relating homotopy to homology in that model, and the structure that survives when an algebra is replaced by its cohomology.

The third is the deepest. Passing to cohomology destroys a differential graded algebra — but not entirely, and what remains is an A∞A_\inftyA∞​-structure whose higher operations are, in the classical language, Massey products.

31.1 Lie models

Theorem 31.1 (Quillen; recalled). The rational homotopy category of simply connected spaces is equivalent to the homotopy category of connected differential graded Lie algebras over Q\QQQ. Under the equivalence a space XXX corresponds to a dg Lie algebra LXL_XLX​ with H∗(LX)≅π∗(ΩX)⊗QH_*(L_X)\cong\pi_*(\Omega X)\otimes\QQH∗​(LX​)≅π∗​(ΩX)⊗Q, the homotopy Lie algebra of Definition 25.5.

So there are two algebraic models of a rational homotopy type: the commutative one of Chapter 24 and the Lie one here. They are related by duality, and each is convenient for different questions — the commutative model computes cohomology, the Lie model computes homotopy.

Exercise 31.2 (medium). Let LLL be a graded Lie algebra. Show that

Δ(x)=x⊗1+1⊗x(x∈L)\Delta(x)=x\otimes1+1\otimes x\qquad(x\in L)Δ(x)=x⊗1+1⊗x(x∈L)

extends to a map of algebras U(L)→U(L)⊗U(L)U(L)\to U(L)\otimes U(L)U(L)→U(L)⊗U(L), making U(L)U(L)U(L) a Hopf algebra in which every element of LLL is primitive.

Hint. By the universal property of UUU, it is enough to check that x↦x⊗1+1⊗xx\mapsto x\otimes1+1\otimes xx↦x⊗1+1⊗x is a map of graded Lie algebras into the commutator Lie algebra of U(L)⊗U(L)U(L)\otimes U(L)U(L)⊗U(L).

Solution. Write a=x⊗1a=x\otimes1a=x⊗1 and b=1⊗yb=1\otimes yb=1⊗y. In the graded tensor product of algebras ab=x⊗yab=x\otimes yab=x⊗y, while ba=(1⊗y)(x⊗1)=(−1)∣x∣∣y∣ x⊗yba=(1\otimes y)(x\otimes1)=(-1)^{|x||y|}\,x\otimes yba=(1⊗y)(x⊗1)=(−1)∣x∣∣y∣x⊗y, so the graded commutator is

[a,b]=ab−(−1)∣a∣∣b∣ba=x⊗y−(−1)∣x∣∣y∣(−1)∣x∣∣y∣x⊗y=0.[a,b]=ab-(-1)^{|a||b|}ba =x\otimes y-(-1)^{|x||y|}(-1)^{|x||y|}x\otimes y=0 .[a,b]=ab−(−1)∣a∣∣b∣ba=x⊗y−(−1)∣x∣∣y∣(−1)∣x∣∣y∣x⊗y=0.

The cross terms therefore vanish, and

[Δx,Δy]=[x,y]⊗1+1⊗[x,y]=Δ[x,y].\big[\Delta x,\Delta y\big]=[x,y]\otimes1+1\otimes[x,y]=\Delta[x,y].[Δx,Δy]=[x,y]⊗1+1⊗[x,y]=Δ[x,y].

So Δ\DeltaΔ is a map of Lie algebras L→(U(L)⊗U(L))LieL\to\big(U(L)\otimes U(L)\big)_{\mathrm{Lie}}L→(U(L)⊗U(L))Lie​, and the universal property of UUU extends it uniquely to an algebra map on U(L)U(L)U(L). Coassociativity and counitality are checked on LLL, where they are immediate, and hold on U(L)U(L)U(L) because both sides are algebra maps agreeing on generators. Every x∈Lx\in Lx∈L is primitive by construction.

Remark 31.3 (Other properties of UUU). Exercise 31.2 is one of a family. The same style of argument — compare universal properties, never compute — identifies UUU of an abelian Lie algebra, describes UUU of a direct sum, and produces a presentation of U(L)U(L)U(L) by generators and relations. Exercise 25.9 was another member of the family, needed in Chapter 25 for the growth estimate.

One of these deserves a warning in advance. The natural guess for U(L⊕M)U(L\oplus M)U(L⊕M) is U(L)⊗U(M)U(L)\otimes U(M)U(L)⊗U(M), and the guess is correct — but not because “left adjoints preserve coproducts”. The coproduct of associative algebras is the free product, not the tensor product; the tensor product is the coproduct in the category of cocommutative Hopf algebras, which is where Exercise 31.2 places U(L)U(L)U(L). Anyone deriving the identity should say which category the coproduct is being taken in.

31.2 The rational Hurewicz map

Definition 31.4. The rational Hurewicz map is the rationalisation of the classical one,

h ⁣:πk(X)⊗Q⟶Hk(X;Q).h\colon\pi_k(X)\otimes\QQ\longrightarrow H_k(X;\QQ).h:πk​(X)⊗Q⟶Hk​(X;Q).

By Theorem 24.16 the source is Hom⁡(Vk,Q)\Hom(V^k,\QQ)Hom(Vk,Q) for the minimal model (ΛV,d)(\Lambda V,d)(ΛV,d), and the target is dual to Hk(ΛV,d)H^k(\Lambda V,d)Hk(ΛV,d). So hhh is dual to a map Hk(ΛV,d)→VkH^k(\Lambda V,d)\to V^kHk(ΛV,d)→Vk, and there is only one natural candidate: the projection of ΛV\Lambda VΛV onto its indecomposables V=Λ≥1V/Λ≥2VV=\Lambda^{\ge1}V/\Lambda^{\ge2}VV=Λ≥1V/Λ≥2V.

Example 31.5 (The two-sphere). Take X=S2X=S^2X=S2, with minimal model (Λ(x,y),dy=x2)(\Lambda(x,y),dy=x^2)(Λ(x,y),dy=x2), ∣x∣=2|x|=2∣x∣=2, ∣y∣=3|y|=3∣y∣=3, from Example 24.5.

On the topological side, π2(S2)⊗Q=Q\pi_2(S^2)\otimes\QQ=\QQπ2​(S2)⊗Q=Q maps isomorphically to H2(S2;Q)=QH_2(S^2;\QQ)=\QQH2​(S2;Q)=Q — the Hurewicz theorem in the bottom degree — while π3(S2)⊗Q=Q\pi_3(S^2)\otimes\QQ=\QQπ3​(S2)⊗Q=Q, generated by the Hopf map, maps to H3(S2;Q)=0H_3(S^2;\QQ)=0H3​(S2;Q)=0, so hhh vanishes in degree three.

On the algebraic side, Exercise 24.6 computed H∗(ΛV,d)=Q{1,x}H^*(\Lambda V,d)=\QQ\{1,x\}H∗(ΛV,d)=Q{1,x}. The projection onto indecomposables carries the class of xxx in H2H^2H2 to x∈V2x\in V^2x∈V2, an isomorphism; and H3=0H^3=0H3=0 maps to V3=QyV^3=\QQ yV3=Qy by the zero map.

The two computations agree degree by degree, and the vanishing in degree three records on one side that H3(S2)=0H_3(S^2)=0H3​(S2)=0 and on the other that yyy is not a cocycle, dy=x2≠0dy=x^2\neq0dy=x2=0. The Hopf map is invisible to homology for exactly the reason that yyy had to be adjoined.

Remark 31.6 (Proving the duality, and what it gives). Establishing the duality in general is the natural exercise here. Both maps are natural, Example 31.5 checks the smallest case, and the pairing between H∗(ΛV,d)H^*(\Lambda V,d)H∗(ΛV,d) and H∗(X;Q)H_*(X;\QQ)H∗​(X;Q) comes from Theorem 24.1; what has to be done is to check the identification on generators, and then to see that minimality is what makes the indecomposable quotient the right target.

The consequence worth having is the Cartan–Serre theorem. If XXX is elliptic in the sense of Definition 25.13 then VVV is finite-dimensional, so only finitely many degrees carry rational homotopy at all; the dichotomy of Theorem 25.15 becomes a statement about how many generators a minimal model can have. Rational homotopy theory turns a question about infinitely many homotopy groups into a question about the size of one graded vector space.

31.3 Transferred structures and Massey products

Theorem 31.7 (Homotopy transfer; recalled). Let (A,dA)(A,d_A)(A,dA​) be a dg algebra and let (H,0)(H,0)(H,0) be a complex equipped with a deformation retraction of AAA onto HHH: maps i ⁣:H→Ai\colon H\to Ai:H→A, p ⁣:A→Hp\colon A\to Hp:A→H and a homotopy hhh with pi=idpi=\mathrm{id}pi=id and ip−id=dAh+hdAip-\mathrm{id}=d_Ah+hd_Aip−id=dA​h+hdA​. Then HHH carries an A∞A_\inftyA∞​-structure whose m2m_2m2​ is the transported product and whose higher operations are built from hhh, in such a way that iii extends to an ∞\infty∞-quasi-isomorphism.

Taking H=H∗(A)H=H^*(A)H=H∗(A) equips the cohomology of any dg algebra with such a structure.

So cohomology remembers more than its ring structure: it remembers a family of higher operations m3,m4,…m_3,m_4,\dotsm3​,m4​,…, of the degrees computed in Example 30.7. The classical name for the first of them is older than the operadic language.

Example 31.10 (Vanishing cup products, nonzero Massey product). Let XXX be the complement of the Borromean rings in S3S^3S3. All cup products of one-dimensional classes vanish — any two of the three circles are unlinked — so the cohomology ring carries no information beyond its groups. The triple Massey product of the three generators is nevertheless nonzero, and it detects the linking that the pairwise products cannot.

This is the phenomenon of Theorem 18.9 again: a ring with nothing in it, and a finer operation that sees the difference. Here the operation is not a Steenrod square but a transferred m3m_3m3​.

Remark 31.11 (What is left, and what it costs). Three statements complete the picture, and each is a reasonable exercise.

First, Definition 31.8 is the transferred m3m_3m3​ of Theorem 31.7. Matching the two means comparing the defining equations of a Massey product with the A∞A_\inftyA∞​ relations of Exercise 30.8: the elements u,vu,vu,v chosen above are values of the homotopy hhh, and the indeterminacy of Exercise 31.9 is the freedom in choosing hhh. Higher Massey products correspond in the same way to m4,m5,…m_4,m_5,\dotsm4​,m5​,…, with signs governed by the Koszul rule.

Second, formality — the existence of a quasi-isomorphism between AAA and its cohomology with zero differential — forces the higher operations, hence all higher Massey products, to vanish.

Third, and this is the caution: the converse fails. Vanishing Massey products do not imply formality, because the transferred structure can be nontrivial in ways that no finite family of products detects. The book states this without proof; what it does supply is the reason the question is subtle, namely that mkm_kmk​ for large kkk carries information no m3m_3m3​ can see.

31.4 Closing

Remark 31.12 (The arc). It is worth looking back along the route.

Chapter 2 could not distinguish two lens spaces; neither could Chapter 4 with homology, Chapter 7 with the cohomology ring, or Chapter 13 with homotopy groups. Chapter 18 produced an operation finer than all of them. Example 31.10 shows the same pattern once more, in a setting where the finer operation is a transferred product rather than a Steenrod square.

The recurring shape is this: an invariant is built, computed until it fails, and the failure names the next invariant. Cup products failed because they are degreewise, and operations repaired that. Strict constructions failed because they are not homotopy invariant, and derived functors repaired that. Homology failed to see the universal cover, and local coefficients repaired that. In every case the repair was already visible in the shape of the failure, which is why the failures were worth studying carefully.

The webapp accompanying this book asks harder questions of the same kind. A reader who has worked the exercises here has the technique for them; what remains is the synthesis, and that is the part no book can do for anyone.

31.5 What to carry forward

  • Two models of a rational homotopy type, commutative and Lie (Theorem 31.1).

  • U(L)U(L)U(L) is a Hopf algebra with LLL primitive (Exercise 31.2); derive properties of UUU from universal properties, minding which category a coproduct is taken in (Remark 31.3).

  • The rational Hurewicz map is dual to the projection onto indecomposables (Example 31.5 and Remark 31.6), whence Cartan–Serre.

  • Cohomology carries a transferred A∞A_\inftyA∞​-structure (Theorem 31.7); its m3m_3m3​ is the triple Massey product (Definition 31.8), a coset and not an element (Exercise 31.9).

  • Formality kills the higher operations; their vanishing does not imply formality (Remark 31.11).
