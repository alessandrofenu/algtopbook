Chapter 30

Operads, Koszul duality, and twisting morphisms

In this chapter

  1. 30.1 S\mathbb{S}S-modules and Schur functors
  2. 30.2 Koszul duality and the weight grading
  3. 30.3 Twisting morphisms and Maurer–Cartan
  4. 30.4 What to carry forward

Chapter 25 defined operads and used them lightly. This chapter makes the composition product explicit, introduces the duality that produces “algebras up to homotopy”, and identifies the equation governing all of it.

The organising observation is that one equation — Maurer–Cartan — appears in three disguises: as the condition defining a twisting morphism, as the condition that a perturbed differential squares to zero, and as the structure equations of a homotopy algebra. Section 30.3 exhibits the first two as the same thing in the case everyone already knows.

30.1 S\mathbb{S}S-modules and Schur functors

Definition 30.1. An S\mathbb{S}S-module is a sequence E=(E(n))n≥0E=(E(n))_{n\ge0}E=(E(n))n≥0​ of representations of the symmetric groups Sn\mathbb{S}_nSn​. Its Schur functor is

E~(V)=⨁n≥0E(n)⊗SnV⊗n,\widetilde E(V)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}E(n)\otimes_{\mathbb{S}_n}V^{\otimes n},E(V)=n≥0⨁​E(n)⊗Sn​​V⊗n,

where Sn\mathbb{S}_nSn​ permutes the tensor factors.

Example 30.2 (The dictionary). Three cases fix the pattern.

  • E(n)E(n)E(n) the trivial representation: E~(V)=⨁nSymn(V)\widetilde E(V)= \bigoplus_n\mathrm{Sym}^n(V)E(V)=⨁n​Symn(V), since coinvariants under the permutation action are the symmetric powers.

  • E(n)E(n)E(n) the sign representation: E~(V)=⨁nΛn(V)\widetilde E(V)=\bigoplus_n\Lambda^n(V)E(V)=⨁n​Λn(V).

  • E(n)=k[Sn]E(n)=k[\mathbb{S}_n]E(n)=k[Sn​] the regular representation: k[Sn]⊗SnV⊗n=V⊗nk[\mathbb{S}_n]\otimes_{\mathbb{S}_n}V^{\otimes n}=V^{\otimes n}k[Sn​]⊗Sn​​V⊗n=V⊗n, so E~(V)=T(V)\widetilde E(V)=T(V)E(V)=T(V), the tensor algebra.

Compare Example 25.2: the three S\mathbb{S}S-modules underlying Com⁡\ComCom, its signed variant, and Ass⁡\AssAss produce exactly the free commutative, exterior and associative algebras.

Definition 30.3. The composition product E∘FE\circ FE∘F of two S\mathbb{S}S-modules is the S\mathbb{S}S-module whose Schur functor is the composite: E∘F~=E~∘F~\widetilde{E\circ F}=\widetilde E\circ\widetilde FE∘F=E∘F. An operad is a monoid for ∘\circ∘, with the multiplication encoding the ∘i\circ_i∘i​ of Definition 25.1.

Remark 30.4 (Making ∘\circ∘ explicit). Definition 30.3 defines E∘FE\circ FE∘F by a property rather than a formula, which is enough for the theory and useless for computation. Writing down the representation (E∘F)(N)(E\circ F)(N)(E∘F)(N) explicitly means expanding E~(F~(V))\widetilde E(\widetilde F(V))E(F(V)) and collecting the terms of tensor degree NNN.

Two features of that expansion are worth predicting before doing it. Each term involves nnn copies of some F(m)F(m)F(m) together with one element of E(n)E(n)E(n), so N=mnN=mnN=mn in the homogeneous case; and the symmetric group SN\mathbb{S}_NSN​ acts by permuting the mmm inputs inside each block and permuting the nnn blocks, which is the action of a wreath product rather than of a direct product. Getting that second point wrong — using Sm×n\mathbb{S}_m^{\times n}Sm×n​ where the wreath product belongs — is the standard error, and it produces a representation of the wrong dimension.

Carrying out the expansion is the natural exercise, and the resulting operation on representations is classical enough to have its own name.

30.2 Koszul duality and the weight grading

Definition 30.5. For a quadratic operad P\mathcal{P}P — one presented by generators of arity two and relations of arity three — there is a Koszul dual cooperad P!‘\mathcal{P}^{\text{!`}}P!‘, and the cobar construction on it,

P∞=Ω P!‘,\mathcal{P}_\infty=\Omega\,\mathcal{P}^{\text{!`}},P∞​=ΩP!‘,

is a cofibrant resolution of P\mathcal{P}P. An algebra over P∞\mathcal{P}_\inftyP∞​ is a P\mathcal{P}P-algebra up to homotopy.

The construction comes with a grading that makes everything computable.

Proposition 30.6 (The weight grading; recalled). P!‘\mathcal{P}^{\text{!`}}P!‘ is graded by weight, the number of generators used, and its weight-www component sits in homological degree w−1w-1w−1. Consequently the operation of P∞\mathcal{P}_\inftyP∞​ coming from weight www raises degree by w−2w-2w−2.

Example 30.7 (A∞A_\inftyA∞​-algebras). Take P=Ass⁡\mathcal{P}=\AssP=Ass. Then P∞\mathcal{P}_\inftyP∞​ is the A∞A_\inftyA∞​-operad, and an algebra over it is a graded module AAA with operations

mk ⁣:A⊗k⟶A,k≥1,m_k\colon A^{\otimes k}\longrightarrow A,\qquad k\ge1,mk​:A⊗k⟶A,k≥1,

of degree k−2k-2k−2, satisfying the relations ∑±mi(1⊗a⊗mj⊗1⊗b)=0\sum\pm m_i(1^{\otimes a}\otimes m_j\otimes1^{\otimes b})=0∑±mi​(1⊗a⊗mj​⊗1⊗b)=0. The first three are familiar: m1m_1m1​ has degree −1-1−1 and squares to zero, so it is a differential; m2m_2m2​ has degree 000 and is a product; m3m_3m3​ has degree 111 and is a homotopy witnessing associativity of m2m_2m2​.

The degrees are forced by Proposition 30.6, and they are the whole content of the definition: once one knows ∣mk∣=k−2|m_k|=k-2∣mk​∣=k−2, the shape of the theory is determined.

Exercise 30.8 (easy). In Example 30.7, write out the relation involving m1m_1m1​, m2m_2m2​ and m3m_3m3​ and identify what each term says.

Solution. The relation in arity two says m1m2=m2(m1⊗1+1⊗m1)m_1m_2=m_2(m_1\otimes1+1\otimes m_1)m1​m2​=m2​(m1​⊗1+1⊗m1​) up to sign: m1m_1m1​ is a derivation for m2m_2m2​, i.e. the Leibniz rule.

The relation in arity three says

m2(m2⊗1−1⊗m2)=±(m1m3+m3(m1⊗1⊗1+⋯)),m_2\big(m_2\otimes1-1\otimes m_2\big) =\pm\Big(m_1m_3+m_3\big(m_1\otimes1\otimes1+\cdots\big)\Big),m2​(m2​⊗1−1⊗m2​)=±(m1​m3​+m3​(m1​⊗1⊗1+⋯)),

whose left side is the associator of m2m_2m2​ and whose right side is the differential of m3m_3m3​. So m2m_2m2​ is associative up to the explicit homotopy m3m_3m3​, and strictly associative exactly when that differential vanishes.

Remark 30.9 (Degree counting as a prediction). Proposition 30.6 lets one predict the shape of a homotopy structure before computing anything. Suppose the underlying module is concentrated in a single degree, with zero differential; then its endomorphism operad is concentrated in degree zero, and an operation of nonzero degree has nowhere to land.

Reading that off for a general P\mathcal{P}P, and identifying exactly which operations survive, is the natural exercise here; Example 30.7 supplies the case P=Ass⁡\mathcal{P}=\AssP=Ass, where ∣mk∣=k−2|m_k|=k-2∣mk​∣=k−2 makes the answer visible at a glance. The one thing to be careful about is the differential: an P∞\mathcal{P}_\inftyP∞​-structure includes m1m_1m1​, and concluding anything requires accounting for it as well as for the higher operations.

30.3 Twisting morphisms and Maurer–Cartan

Definition 30.10. Let C\mathcal{C}C be a coaugmented cooperad and AAA a dg module. The graded space Hom⁡S(C,End⁡A)\Hom_{\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{C},\End_A)HomS​(C,EndA​) carries a convolution product ⋆\star⋆, formed by comultiplying in C\mathcal{C}C, applying the two maps, and composing in End⁡A\End_AEndA​, together with a differential ∂\partial∂ induced by those of C\mathcal{C}C and AAA. A degree −1-1−1 element α\alphaα with α∘η=0\alpha\circ\eta=0α∘η=0 is a twisting morphism if it satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation

∂(α)+α⋆α=0.\partial(\alpha)+\alpha\star\alpha=0 .∂(α)+α⋆α=0.

The equation is not exotic. It is the condition, familiar from deformation theory, under which a perturbed differential still squares to zero.

Exercise 30.11 (medium). Let (g,d,[ , ])(\mathfrak{g},d,[\,,\,])(g,d,[,]) be a differential graded Lie algebra and x∈g1x\in\mathfrak{g}^1x∈g1. Show that

satisfies dx2=0d_x^2=0dx2​=0 if and only if dx+12[x,x]=0dx+\tfrac12[x,x]=0dx+21​[x,x]=0.

Hint. Expand dx2(y)d_x^2(y)dx2​(y) using the Leibniz rule d[x,y]=[dx,y]−[x,dy]d[x,y]=[dx,y]-[x,dy]d[x,y]=[dx,y]−[x,dy], valid because ∣x∣=1|x|=1∣x∣=1, and then use the graded Jacobi identity to rewrite [x,[x,y]][x,[x,y]][x,[x,y]].

Solution. For y∈gy\in\mathfrak{g}y∈g,

dx2(y)=d(dy+[x,y])+[x, dy+[x,y]]=d[x,y]+[x,dy]+[x,[x,y]].d_x^2(y)=d\big(dy+[x,y]\big)+\big[x,\ dy+[x,y]\big] =d[x,y]+[x,dy]+\big[x,[x,y]\big].dx2​(y)=d(dy+[x,y])+[x, dy+[x,y]]=d[x,y]+[x,dy]+[x,[x,y]].

Since ∣x∣=1|x|=1∣x∣=1 the Leibniz rule reads d[x,y]=[dx,y]−[x,dy]d[x,y]=[dx,y]-[x,dy]d[x,y]=[dx,y]−[x,dy], so the middle terms cancel and

dx2(y)=[dx,y]+[x,[x,y]].d_x^2(y)=[dx,y]+\big[x,[x,y]\big].dx2​(y)=[dx,y]+[x,[x,y]].

The graded Jacobi identity for an odd element gives [x,[x,y]]=12[[x,x],y][x,[x,y]]=\tfrac12\big[[x,x],y\big][x,[x,y]]=21​[[x,x],y]. Hence

dx2(y)=[ dx+12[x,x], y ],d_x^2(y)=\Big[\,dx+\tfrac12[x,x],\ y\,\Big],dx2​(y)=[dx+21​[x,x], y],

which vanishes for all yyy exactly when dx+12[x,x]=0dx+\tfrac12[x,x]=0dx+21​[x,x]=0.

Remark 30.12 (The same statement, operadically). Exercise 30.11 says that a Maurer–Cartan element is precisely a perturbation of a differential that preserves the property of squaring to zero. The operadic version of Definition 30.10 says the same thing, and setting it up is a matter of bookkeeping: extend a twisting morphism α\alphaα to a map α~\tilde\alphaα~ defined on all of C\mathcal{C}C by declaring its value on the coaugmentation to be the differential dAd_AdA​, and then compare α~⋆α~\tilde\alpha\star\tilde\alphaα~⋆α~ with ∂(α)+α⋆α\partial(\alpha)+\alpha\star\alpha∂(α)+α⋆α.

Two things make that comparison delicate, and both are worth anticipating. The terms of α~⋆α~\tilde\alpha\star\tilde\alphaα~⋆α~ split according to where the coaugmentation lands, and only some of them reproduce ∂α\partial\alpha∂α; and the signs are governed by the Koszul rule of Lemma 28.8, so they have to be tracked rather than guessed. Carrying it out is the natural exercise, and the payoff is that twisting morphisms, the bar–cobar adjunction and ∞\infty∞-morphisms all become instances of one equation.

Remark 30.13 (Forward). Two threads run into Chapter 31. The homotopy transfer theorem — that a P∞\mathcal{P}_\inftyP∞​-structure can be pushed along a homotopy equivalence of underlying complexes — is proved by producing a twisting morphism, so it is an application of Section 30.3. And the A∞A_\inftyA∞​-structures of Example 30.7, transferred onto cohomology, turn out to be a classical construction in disguise.

30.4 What to carry forward

  • Schur functors and the dictionary (Definition 30.1 and Example 30.2).

  • The composition product is composition of Schur functors (Definition 30.3); making it explicit involves a wreath product (Remark 30.4).

  • Weight grading fixes the degrees of a homotopy structure (Proposition 30.6); for A∞A_\inftyA∞​, ∣mk∣=k−2|m_k|=k-2∣mk​∣=k−2 (Example 30.7 and Exercise 30.8).

  • Maurer–Cartan is the condition for a perturbed differential to square to zero (Exercise 30.11), and the operadic form is the same statement (Definition 30.10 and Remark 30.12).
