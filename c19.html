Chapter 19

Rational homotopy: the first computations

In this chapter

  2. 19.2 Whitehead products

Over Q\QQQ the machinery of the last four chapters becomes decisive. Torsion disappears, the universal coefficient theorem loses its Ext⁡\ExtExt term, and the Serre spectral sequence — whose differentials are usually determined only up to extension — becomes a computation in graded-commutative algebra over a field. Whole homotopy types become finite pieces of algebraic data, and Chapter 24 will make that statement precise.

This chapter does the computations that come first: the rational cohomology of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces, the transgression technique that produces them, and the Whitehead product, which is where the even-dimensional spheres reveal that they carry more rational homotopy than one expects.

19.1 Eilenberg–MacLane spaces over Q\QQQ

Theorem 19.1 (Recalled). For n≥1n\ge1n≥1,

H∗(K(Z,n);Q)={Q[xn],n even,Λ(xn),n odd,∣xn∣=n.H^*\big(K(\ZZ,n);\QQ\big)= \begin{cases} \QQ[x_n],&n\ \text{even},\\ \Lambda(x_n),&n\ \text{odd}, \end{cases} \qquad |x_n|=n .H∗(K(Z,n);Q)={Q[xn​],Λ(xn​),​n even,n odd,​∣xn​∣=n.

Moreover H∗(K(π,n);Q)=QH^*(K(\pi,n);\QQ)=\QQH∗(K(π,n);Q)=Q for every finite group π\piπ.

The parity is not a curiosity: it is graded commutativity. A generator of even degree may be raised to arbitrary powers, while one of odd degree squares to zero, and the two cases produce a polynomial algebra and an exterior algebra respectively. Almost every parity condition in rational homotopy theory traces back to this.

Example 19.2 (Degree two). By Exercise 14.6 we have K(Z,2)≃CP∞K(\ZZ,2)\simeq\CP^\inftyK(Z,2)≃CP∞, and Example 16.2 computed H∗(K(Z,2);Z)=Z[c]H^*(K(\ZZ,2);\ZZ)=\ZZ[c]H∗(K(Z,2);Z)=Z[c]. Tensoring with Q\QQQ leaves Q[c]\QQ[c]Q[c], which is the even case of Theorem 19.1.

19.3 The rational homotopy of spheres

Theorem 19.10 (Serre; recalled, not proved here). For n≥2n\ge2n≥2,

πk(Sn)⊗Q={Q,k=n,Q,k=2n−1 and n even,0,otherwise.\pi_k(S^n)\otimes\QQ= \begin{cases} \QQ,&k=n,\\ \QQ,&k=2n-1\ \text{and}\ n\ \text{even},\\ 0,&\text{otherwise.} \end{cases}πk​(Sn)⊗Q=⎩⎨⎧​Q,Q,0,​k=n,k=2n−1 and n even,otherwise.​

Warning 19.11. The book does not prove Theorem 19.10, and the reader should not take the surrounding computations as a proof of it. The standard argument compares SnS^nSn with K(Z,n)K(\ZZ,n)K(Z,n) and, for nnn even, kills the class xn2x_n^2xn2​ by passing to the homotopy fibre of a map K(Z,n)→K(Z,2n)K(\ZZ,n)\to K(\ZZ,2n)K(Z,n)→K(Z,2n); computing the rational cohomology of that fibre is exactly the step this chapter leaves out. Everything needed for it is nevertheless in place: Theorem 19.1 gives the two cohomologies, Exercise 19.3 shows how a transgression kills a polynomial generator, and Example 16.2 shows how multiplicativity propagates a single differential through a whole ring.

Remark 19.12 (Where the extra class comes from). For nnn even, Theorem 19.10 produces a rational homotopy class in degree 2n−12n-12n−1 that odd spheres do not have. 19.4 What to carry forward

  • H∗(K(Z,n);Q)H^*(K(\ZZ,n);\QQ)H∗(K(Z,n);Q) is polynomial for nnn even and exterior for nnn odd (Theorem 19.1); the parity is graded commutativity and nothing else.

  • Transgression plus the Leibniz rule computes a whole ring from one differential (Exercises 19.3 and 19.4); over Q\QQQ the integer factors that obstructed the integral computation become invertible.

  • An even sphere’s loop space needs an extra generator (Warning 19.5), and the Whitehead square is what produces it (Definition 19.6 and Remark 19.9).

  • (a+b)2=(1+(−1)n)ab(a+b)^2=(1+(-1)^n)ab(a+b)2=(1+(−1)n)ab (Exercise 19.8) — two lines of graded commutativity that decide the whole parity story.

  • Serre’s theorem (Theorem 19.10) is quoted, not proved (Warning 19.11).
