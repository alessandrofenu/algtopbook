Chapter 23 defined quasi-categories and stopped. This chapter assembles the three tools one needs to work with them: an induction that upgrades lifting against generators to lifting against all monomorphisms; an explicit description of the simplicial category associated with a simplex; and a cofinality criterion that replaces explicit contracting homotopies by a statement about comma categories.

None of the three is deep. All three are the kind of thing that, unstated, makes the literature unreadable.

29.1 Lifting against everything

Definition 29.1. A map of simplicial sets is a trivial fibration if it has the right lifting property against the boundary inclusions ∂Δn↪Δn\partial\Delta^n\hookrightarrow\Delta^n∂Δn↪Δn for all n≥0n\ge0n≥0.

Exercise 29.2 (medium). Show that a trivial fibration lifts against all monomorphisms, not merely against the boundary inclusions of Definition 29.1.

Hint. A monomorphism A↪BA\hookrightarrow BA↪B is built from AAA by attaching nondegenerate simplices of BBB that are not in AAA, one dimension at a time. What is the pushout describing the attachment of a single such simplex?

Solution. Filter BBB by A=B(−1)⊆B(0)⊆⋯A=B^{(-1)}\subseteq B^{(0)}\subseteq\cdotsA=B(−1)⊆B(0)⊆⋯, where B(n)B^{(n)}B(n) is AAA together with all simplices of BBB of dimension at most nnn. Passing from B(n−1)B^{(n-1)}B(n−1) to B(n)B^{(n)}B(n) attaches the nondegenerate nnn-simplices of BBB not in AAA, and each attachment is a pushout

∐∂Δn \coprod\partial\Delta^n ∐∂Δn
B(n−1) B^{(n-1)}B(n−1)
∐Δn \coprod\Delta^n ∐Δn
B(n). B^{(n)} . B(n).

The right lifting property is preserved under cobase change — this is Exercise 22.7 read on the other side — and under composition and transfinite composition. Since ppp lifts against each ∂Δn↪Δn\partial\Delta^n\hookrightarrow\Delta^n∂Δn↪Δn, it lifts against every stage of the filtration, hence against A↪BA\hookrightarrow BA↪B.

Remark 29.3 (What the strong form is for). The typical application is to build an inverse. Given a trivial fibration p ⁣:X→Yp\colon X\to Yp:X→Y and a quasi-category ZZZ, one wants to invert the map [Y,Z]→[X,Z][Y,Z]\to[X,Z][Y,Z]→[X,Z] induced by ppp; constructing the inverse means solving a lifting problem whose left-hand map is ∅→Y\varnothing\to Y∅→Y or, for the homotopy statement, an inclusion A×J∪B×∂J↪B×JA\times J\cup B\times\partial J\hookrightarrow B\times JA×J∪B×∂J↪B×J. Neither is a boundary inclusion, and neither is a generator; both are monomorphisms.

So Exercise 29.2 is exactly what converts Definition 29.1 into a statement about mapping spaces, and anyone attempting such an argument with only the generators available will stall at the first step. Establishing that trivial fibrations induce bijections on homotopy classes of maps into any quasi-category — that is, that they are equivalences in the appropriate sense — is the natural exercise here, and its only ingredient beyond this section is a description of homotopies of simplicial maps.

29.2 Rigidification

The comparison between quasi-categories and categories enriched in simplicial sets goes through an adjunction

C  ⁣: sSet ⇄ sCat  ⁣: Nhc,\mathfrak{C}\ \colon\ \mathbf{sSet}\ \rightleftarrows\ \mathbf{sCat}\ \colon\ N_{\mathrm{hc}},C : sSet ⇄ sCat : Nhc​,

where NhcN_{\mathrm{hc}}Nhc​ is the homotopy coherent nerve. What makes it computable is an explicit formula for C\mathfrak{C}C on simplices.

Proposition 29.4 (The hom-cube; recalled). For the standard simplex,

CΔn(i,j)=N(Pi,j),Pi,j={ S⊆{i,i+1,…,j} : i,j∈S },\mathfrak{C}\Delta^n(i,j)=N\big(P_{i,j}\big),\qquad P_{i,j}=\big\{\,S\subseteq\{i,i+1,\dots,j\}\ :\ i,j\in S\,\big\},CΔn(i,j)=N(Pi,j​),Pi,j​={S⊆{i,i+1,…,j} : i,j∈S},

the nerve of that poset ordered by inclusion, and empty when i>ji>ji>j. It is a cube of dimension j−i−1j-i-1j−i−1.

Example 29.5 (Small cases). For n=1n=1n=1 and (i,j)=(0,1)(i,j)=(0,1)(i,j)=(0,1) the poset P0,1P_{0,1}P0,1​ has the single element {0,1}\{0,1\}{0,1}, so CΔ1(0,1)=Δ0\mathfrak{C}\Delta^1(0,1)=\Delta^0CΔ1(0,1)=Δ0: a point, a cube of dimension 1−0−1=01-0-1=01−0−1=0. Hence CΔ1\mathfrak{C}\Delta^1CΔ1 is the category [1][1][1] with discrete hom-spaces.

For n=2n=2n=2 and (i,j)=(0,2)(i,j)=(0,2)(i,j)=(0,2) the poset has two elements, {0,2}⊂{0,1,2}\{0,2\}\subset \{0,1,2\}{0,2}⊂{0,1,2}, so CΔ2(0,2)=Δ1\mathfrak{C}\Delta^2(0,2)=\Delta^1CΔ2(0,2)=Δ1: an interval, a cube of dimension 111. The two endpoints are the two ways of getting from 000 to 222 — directly, or via 111 — and the interval between them is the homotopy relating them.

For n=3n=3n=3 and (i,j)=(0,3)(i,j)=(0,3)(i,j)=(0,3) the poset has four elements and the hom-space is a square.

Warning 29.6. Example 29.5 is worth reading twice. Already for Δ2\Delta^2Δ2 the hom-space from 000 to 222 is an interval and not a point: the composite is not unique, it is unique up to a specified homotopy. That is the entire difference between CΔn\mathfrak{C}\Delta^nCΔn and the poset [n][n][n], and it is why C\mathfrak{C}C is called rigidification — it produces a genuinely simplicial category, not a discrete one.

Proposition 29.7 (Rigidifying a nerve; recalled). Let A\mathcal{A}A be an ordinary category. Then C(NA)\mathfrak{C}(N\mathcal{A})C(NA) is the free simplicial resolution of A\mathcal{A}A. In the hom-space between two objects, a vertex is a chain of composable arrows, none of them an identity, running from the first object to the second; the simplices above dimension zero record iterated bracketings of such a chain.

Remark 29.8 (A left adjoint that fails to preserve products). C\mathfrak{C}C is a left adjoint, so by Exercise 20.2 it preserves colimits. Warning 20.3 warned that this says nothing about products, and C\mathfrak{C}C is the promised example.

To see it, compare two computations of the same hom-space. On one side, CΔ1×CΔ1\mathfrak{C}\Delta^1\times\mathfrak{C}\Delta^1CΔ1×CΔ1 is a product of two copies of [1][1][1] with discrete homs, by Example 29.5, so its hom-space between the two extreme objects is a single point. On the other, Δ1×Δ1\Delta^1\times\Delta^1Δ1×Δ1 is the nerve of the commuting square, so Proposition 29.7 applies and the corresponding hom-space has one 000-simplex for each string of composable non-identity arrows from one corner to the opposite one.

Counting those strings settles the matter, and the count is the exercise. What should be noted afterwards is that the comparison map is nevertheless a weak equivalence: the failure is strict, not homotopical, which is exactly the situation Chapter 22 was built to handle.

29.3 Cofinality, and extra degeneracies

Definition 29.9. A functor u ⁣:J′→Ju\colon J'\to Ju:J′→J is homotopy initial if for every j∈Jj\in Jj∈J the comma category (u↓j)(u\downarrow j)(u↓j) has contractible nerve. Homotopy colimits may then be computed over J′J'J′ instead of JJJ.

Example 29.10 (The bar resolution, by hand). Let GGG be a group and let E∙GE_\bullet GE∙​G be the simplicial set with EnG=Gn+1E_nG=G^{n+1}En​G=Gn+1 and the evident faces and degeneracies. It is augmented over a point, and it admits an extra degeneracy

s−1 ⁣:EnG⟶En+1G,(g0,…,gn)⟼(e,g0,…,gn),s_{-1}\colon E_nG\longrightarrow E_{n+1}G,\qquad (g_0,\dots,g_n)\longmapsto(e,g_0,\dots,g_n),s−1​:En​G⟶En+1​G,(g0​,…,gn​)⟼(e,g0​,…,gn​),

satisfying the simplicial identities that make it a contracting homotopy for the augmented complex.

The classical consequence is that ∣E∙G∣|E_\bullet G|∣E∙​G∣ is contractible: the extra degeneracy provides, degree by degree, a homotopy from the identity to the constant map. That is what made ∣E∙G∣→BG|E_\bullet G|\to BG∣E∙​G∣→BG a universal cover in Exercise 20.18.

Exercise 29.11 (medium). Let A∙A_\bulletA∙​ be an augmented simplicial abelian group with an extra degeneracy. Show that its associated chain complex is acyclic in positive degrees, with H0H_0H0​ the augmentation.

Hint. Write h=(−1)n+1s−1h=(-1)^{n+1}s_{-1}h=(−1)n+1s−1​ on degree nnn and compute ∂h+h∂\partial h+h\partial∂h+h∂ using the simplicial identities.

Solution. With ∂=∑i(−1)idi\partial=\sum_i(-1)^id_i∂=∑i​(−1)idi​ and h=±s−1h=\pm s_{-1}h=±s−1​, the simplicial identities d0s−1=idd_0s_{-1}=\mathrm{id}d0​s−1​=id and di+1s−1=s−1did_{i+1}s_{-1}=s_{-1}d_idi+1​s−1​=s−1​di​ give, after collecting signs,

∂h+h∂=id\partial h+h\partial=\mathrm{id}∂h+h∂=id

in positive degrees, and in degree zero the same computation leaves the augmentation. A chain homotopy from the identity to zero forces the homology to vanish where it applies.

Remark 29.12 (Replacing the computation by a statement). Example 29.10 and Exercise 29.11 are the hands-on version, and they have to be redone every time the indexing changes. The conceptual version observes that an augmentation together with a contracting homotopy is exactly the data of an extension of the simplicial object over a larger category — the simplex category with a formally adjoined top or bottom element — and that the inclusion of Δ\DeltaΔ into that larger category is homotopy initial in the sense of Definition 29.9.

Once that is established, the conclusion of Example 29.10 is a corollary rather than a computation, and it applies verbatim to bar resolutions, to Čech nerves, and to descent situations, where the hands-on argument would have to be repeated each time. Establishing it is the natural exercise here, and the two things to be careful about are which of the two adjoined elements corresponds to which handedness of contracting homotopy, and which of homotopy initial and homotopy final is the condition relevant to colimits.

29.4 What to carry forward

  • A trivial fibration lifts against every monomorphism, not only the generators (Exercise 29.2); the strong form is what applications use (Remark 29.3).

  • The hom-cube of a simplex (Proposition 29.4), with its small cases worked out (Example 29.5).

  • Already for Δ2\Delta^2Δ2 the hom-space is an interval, not a point (Warning 29.6).

  • Rigidifying a nerve gives the free simplicial resolution (Proposition 29.7).

  • C\mathfrak{C}C preserves colimits but not products (Remark 29.8).

  • Extra degeneracies give contractibility (Example 29.10 and Exercise 29.11); cofinality is how one stops redoing that computation (Definition 29.9 and Remark 29.12).
