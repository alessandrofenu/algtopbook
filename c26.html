Integral characteristic classes

In this chapter

  3. 26.3 Which classes are characteristic classes?

Chapter 12 worked mod 222, where the arithmetic is easy and the conclusions are about stable triviality. This chapter works over Z\ZZZ, where signs and torsion both matter and the conclusions are correspondingly sharper.

The organising object is the Euler class. It reduces mod 222 to the top Stiefel–Whitney class, so it refines the mod-222 theory; it is a torsion class in odd rank, so it is detected by a Bockstein; and it is multiplicative, so it can be computed on sums of line bundles. Those three facts are the three edges of the triangle Remark 12.12 promised, and Section 26.2 draws them.

26.1 The Euler class

Definition 26.1. Let E→BE\to BE→B be an oriented real vector bundle of rank kkk. Its Euler class e(E)∈Hk(B;Z)e(E)\in H^k(B;\ZZ)e(E)∈Hk(B;Z) is the restriction of the Thom class (Theorem 16.12) to the zero section; equivalently it is the obstruction appearing in the Gysin sequence of Theorem 16.9.

Proposition 26.2 (Basic properties; recalled).

  1. Naturality: e(f∗E)=f∗e(E)e(f^*E)=f^*e(E)e(f∗E)=f∗e(E).

  2. Multiplicativity: e(E⊕F)=e(E)⌣e(F)e(E\oplus F)=e(E)\smile e(F)e(E⊕F)=e(E)⌣e(F).

  3. If EEE admits a nowhere-zero section then e(E)=0e(E)=0e(E)=0.

  4. The mod-222 reduction of e(E)e(E)e(E) is the top Stiefel–Whitney class: ρ e(E)=wk(E)\rho\,e(E)=w_k(E)ρe(E)=wk​(E).

Part (c) is the reason for the name: for the tangent bundle of a closed oriented manifold, e(TM)e(TM)e(TM) evaluated on the fundamental class is the Euler characteristic, and it vanishes exactly when a nowhere-zero vector field exists.

e(L)=c1(L)∈H2(B;Z),e(L)=c_1(L)\in H^2(B;\ZZ),e(L)=c1​(L)∈H2(B;Z),

and every class in H2(B;Z)H^2(B;\ZZ)H2(B;Z) arises: line bundles over BBB are in bijection with H2(B;Z)H^2(B;\ZZ)H2(B;Z). So in rank one the realisation question has the simplest possible answer.

Over CP∞\CP^\inftyCP∞ itself the tautological line bundle has e=ce=ce=c, the generator of H2H^2H2, and H∗(CP∞;Z)=Z[c]H^*(\CP^\infty;\ZZ)=\ZZ[c]H∗(CP∞;Z)=Z[c] by Example 16.2.

Exercise 26.4 (easy). Show that e(L⊗L′)=e(L)+e(L′)e(L\otimes L')=e(L)+e(L')e(L⊗L′)=e(L)+e(L′) for complex line bundles.

Hint. Tensor product of line bundles corresponds to a map CP∞×CP∞→CP∞\CP^\infty\times\CP^\infty\to\CP^\inftyCP∞×CP∞→CP∞. Which map, given that CP∞=K(Z,2)\CP^\infty=K(\ZZ,2)CP∞=K(Z,2)?

Solution. Exercise 26.5 (medium). Let EEE be an oriented real vector bundle of odd rank. Show 2 e(E)=02\,e(E)=02e(E)=0.

Hint. Find a bundle automorphism of EEE that reverses the orientation, and apply naturality.

Solution. Let σ ⁣:E→E\sigma\colon E\to Eσ:E→E be fibrewise multiplication by −1-1−1. It is a bundle automorphism covering the identity of BBB, and on each fibre it has determinant (−1)rank=−1(-1)^{\mathrm{rank}}=-1(−1)rank=−1, since the rank is odd. So σ\sigmaσ carries the given orientation to the opposite one.

Reversing the orientation of a bundle negates its Euler class, directly from Definition 26.1: the Thom class changes sign. On the other hand σ\sigmaσ is an isomorphism of bundles, so by naturality it preserves the Euler class. Combining, e(E)=−e(E)e(E)=-e(E)e(E)=−e(E), that is 2e(E)=02e(E)=02e(E)=0.

26.2 The triangle

Remark 12.12 promised a relation between three things: the mod-222 classes wiw_iwi​, the Bockstein of Chapter 9, and the integral Euler class. Here are the three edges.

Edge one: eee reduces to wtopw_{\mathrm{top}}wtop​. This is Proposition 26.2(d). So the mod-222 theory sees the Euler class, but only its reduction; anything of odd order or any sign information is lost.

Edge two: in odd rank eee is 222-torsion. This is Exercise 26.5. A 222-torsion integral class is exactly what the Bockstein produces: the coefficient sequence 0→Z→2Z→F2→00\to\ZZ\xrightarrow{2}\ZZ\to\FF_2\to00→Z2​Z→F2​→0 of Definition 9.11 has connecting map β~ ⁣:Hn(B;F2)→Hn+1(B;Z)\tilde\beta\colon H^{n}(B;\FF_2)\to H^{n+1}(B;\ZZ)β~​:Hn(B;F2​)→Hn+1(B;Z) whose image consists of classes killed by 222, and exactness identifies that image as the kernel of multiplication by 222. So e(E)=β~(u)e(E)=\tilde\beta(u)e(E)=β~​(u) for some mod-222 class uuu, and the question is which one.

Edge three: Wu’s formula. The mod-222 classes are related to each other by a Steenrod operation:

First, the relation between ρβ~\rho\tilde\betaρβ~​ and Sq⁡1\Sq^1Sq1: these are two natural stable operations of degree one, and Remark 18.15 explained why there is only one such and what has to be checked to identify them. 26.3 Which classes are characteristic classes?

Example 26.9 (Rank one is unconstrained). By Example 26.3, every class in H2(B;Z)H^2(B;\ZZ)H2(B;Z) is the Euler class of a line bundle. There is no constraint at all in rank one.

Remark 26.10 (Higher rank). In rank kkk the multiplicativity of Proposition 26.2(b) makes the Euler class of a sum of line bundles a product of kkk classes of degree two. Over CP∞\CP^\inftyCP∞, where H∗=Z[c]H^*=\ZZ[c]H∗=Z[c], the question of which classes of H2kH^{2k}H2k arise this way is therefore a question about which multiples of ckc^kck can be written as a product of kkk classes in Zc\ZZ cZc — and Exercise 26.4 supplies more freedom than one might expect, since e(L⊗m)=m e(L)e(L^{\otimes m})=m\,e(L)e(L⊗m)=me(L).

Working out the answer is a short exercise once those two facts are in hand, and the conclusion is worth contrasting with Remark 26.11 below: the top class is unconstrained, while the total class is not.

Remark 26.11 (The total class is constrained). Not every formal expression 1+c1+c2+⋯1+c_1+c_2+\cdots1+c1​+c2​+⋯ is the total Chern class of a bundle. The standard obstruction is arithmetic: for a complex bundle over S2kS^{2k}S2k, the top Chern class ckc_kck​ evaluated on the fundamental class is divisible by (k−1)!(k-1)!(k−1)!, a theorem of Bott. So realisability of a single class in the top degree, which Example 26.9 and Remark 26.10 make easy, is a quite different question from realisability of a whole system of classes.

This is the integral counterpart of the contrast promised in Remark 12.12, and the reason characteristic classes are studied through classifying spaces: H∗(BU(n);Z)=Z[c1,…,cn]H^*(BU(n);\ZZ)=\ZZ[c_1,\dots,c_n]H∗(BU(n);Z)=Z[c1​,…,cn​] says exactly which universal classes exist, and the constraints appear only when one asks which of them survive pullback along a map from a particular space.

26.4 What to carry forward

  • The Euler class (Definition 26.1) and its four properties (Proposition 26.2); it reduces to wtopw_{\mathrm{top}}wtop​.

  • Line bundles are classified by H2H^2H2, and eee is additive under tensor product (Example 26.3 and Exercise 26.4) — the H-space structure of K(Z,2)K(\ZZ,2)K(Z,2) at work.

  • Odd rank forces 2e=02e=02e=0 (Exercise 26.5), so the odd-rank Euler class lives in the image of a Bockstein.

  • Wu’s formula (Exercise 26.7) relates the mod-222 classes to each other by Sq⁡1\Sq^1Sq1; with the previous two items it closes the triangle of Remark 12.12 up to the identification discussed in Remark 26.8.

  • Top classes are unconstrained; total classes are not (Remark 26.11).
