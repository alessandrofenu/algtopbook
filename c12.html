Chapter 12

Vector bundles and Stiefel–Whitney classes

In this chapter

  1. 12.1 Bundles
  2. 12.2 Stiefel–Whitney classes
  3. 12.3 What the mod-222 theory cannot do
  4. 12.4 What to carry forward

Characteristic classes attach cohomology to a bundle, turning geometric questions — is this bundle trivial? does this manifold admit qqq independent vector fields? — into computations in a ring one already knows. This chapter sets up the mod-222 theory, which is the easiest and the sharpest of its kind, and shows it deciding a question about projective spaces.

The pattern to watch is that a single structural identity about the tangent bundle, fed through a multiplicative axiom, converts a topological question into an arithmetic one about binomial coefficients. That conversion is the whole technique, and Chapter 26 runs it again over Z\ZZZ where the arithmetic is harder and the conclusions are stronger.

12.1 Bundles

Definition 12.1. A real vector bundle of rank nnn over XXX is a map p ⁣:E→Xp\colon E\to Xp:E→X with a real vector space structure on each fibre p−1(x)p^{-1}(x)p−1(x), locally trivial: every point of XXX has a neighbourhood UUU with a homeomorphism p−1(U)≅U×Rnp^{-1}(U)\cong U\times\RR^np−1(U)≅U×Rn that is linear on fibres. We write εn\varepsilon^nεn for the trivial bundle X×RnX\times\RR^nX×Rn.

Bundles pull back along maps, add under Whitney sum E⊕FE\oplus FE⊕F, and are classified: over a paracompact base, isomorphism classes of rank-nnn real bundles correspond to homotopy classes of maps X→BO(n)X\to BO(n)X→BO(n), with the universal bundle pulling back to the given one.

Example 12.2 (The tautological line bundle). Over RPn\RP^nRPn let γ\gammaγ be the bundle whose fibre over a line ℓ⊆Rn+1\ell\subseteq\RR^{n+1}ℓ⊆Rn+1 is ℓ\ellℓ itself. It is the basic example, and it is not trivial: a nowhere-zero section would be a continuous choice of nonzero vector in each line, i.e. a map Sn→Rn+1∖{0}S^n\to\RR^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}Sn→Rn+1∖{0} that is equivariant for the antipodal action up to sign in a way no map can be.

Proposition 12.3. TRPq⊕ε1≅(q+1)γT\RP^q\oplus\varepsilon^1\cong(q+1)\gammaTRPq⊕ε1≅(q+1)γ, the Whitney sum of q+1q+1q+1 copies of the tautological line bundle.

The last statement is what makes the axioms legitimate rather than optimistic: the classes exist because the cohomology of the classifying space is a polynomial ring on them, and every characteristic class is a polynomial in them.

Example 12.5 (Trivial bundles, and orientability). A trivial bundle is pulled back from a point, so by naturality w(εn)=1w(\varepsilon^n)=1w(εn)=1; and by the Whitney formula, adding a trivial summand does not change the total class. Consequently www is an invariant of the stable isomorphism class of a bundle, which is exactly the right level of generality for Proposition 12.3 to be useful.

In rank one, w1w_1w1​ detects orientability: w1(E)=0w_1(E)=0w1​(E)=0 if and only if EEE is orientable. Exercise 12.9 (easy). Deduce from Example 12.8 that RP2\RP^2RP2 is not parallelisable, and indeed that TRP2T\RP^2TRP2 does not become trivial after adding any trivial bundle.

Solution. If TRP2⊕εkT\RP^2\oplus\varepsilon^kTRP2⊕εk were trivial then its total class would be 111 by Example 12.5; but adding a trivial summand does not change www, so w(RP2)w(\RP^2)w(RP2) would be 111, contradicting w(RP2)=1+a+a2w(\RP^2)=1+a+a^2w(RP2)=1+a+a2. In particular TRP2T\RP^2TRP2 itself is not trivial, so RP2\RP^2RP2 is not parallelisable.

Note that RP1=S1\RP^1=S^1RP1=S1 and RP3\RP^3RP3 pass this test, which does not prove they are parallelisable — only that www raises no objection. Both in fact are, being Lie groups.

Remark 12.11 (The question this sets up). Example 12.6 says that w(RPq)=1w(\RP^q)=1w(RPq)=1 exactly when (1+a)q+1=1(1+a)^{q+1}=1(1+a)q+1=1 in F2[a]/(aq+1)\FF_2[a]/(a^{q+1})F2​[a]/(aq+1), i.e. exactly when (q+1i)\binom{q+1}{i}(iq+1​) is even for all 0<i≤q0<i\le q0<i≤q. Exercise 12.10 settles one direction of the resulting arithmetic; the converse needs the general statement about binomial coefficients modulo 222 — Lucas’ theorem, which reads off the parity of (mi)\binom{m}{i}(im​) from a digitwise comparison of the binary expansions of iii and mmm, the coefficient being odd exactly when no digit of iii exceeds the corresponding digit of mmm. Assembling the two halves determines exactly which projective spaces pass the test of Exercise 12.9, and Example 12.8 already shows the pattern in the first three cases.

Two cautions for whoever carries that out. The conclusion concerns stable triviality, since www cannot distinguish TRPqT\RP^qTRPq from TRPq⊕εT\RP^q\oplus\varepsilonTRPq⊕ε; promoting it to parallelisability is a genuinely harder problem, solved by Adams. And the computation must respect Warning 12.7.

12.3 What the mod-222 theory cannot do

Remark 12.12. Stiefel–Whitney classes live in F2\FF_2F2​-cohomology, so they are blind to everything of odd order and to all sign information. Three examples of what they miss, each repaired later:

  • An oriented bundle has an Euler class e(E)∈Hn(X;Z)e(E)\in H^n(X;\ZZ)e(E)∈Hn(X;Z) whose mod-222 reduction is wn(E)w_n(E)wn​(E) but which carries strictly more information; Chapter 26 computes with it.

  • Complex bundles have Chern classes in integral cohomology, and the relation between cic_ici​ and w2iw_{2i}w2i​ is again reduction mod 222.

  • The relation between the two theories is itself a cohomology operation: Sq⁡1wi\Sq^1w_iSq1wi​ is expressible in terms of the wjw_jwj​ by the Wu formula, and Sq⁡1\Sq^1Sq1 is a stable operation of degree one, so it converts mod-222 data into information about integral torsion — compare Chapter 9. That triangle — Stiefel–Whitney, Bockstein, Euler — is what Chapter 26 closes.

  • TRPq⊕ε≅(q+1)γT\RP^q\oplus\varepsilon\cong(q+1)\gammaTRPq⊕ε≅(q+1)γ (Proposition 12.3) — one structural identity, from which everything about w(RPq)w(\RP^q)w(RPq) follows.

  • The axioms (Theorem 12.4), and the fact that www is a stable invariant (Example 12.5).

  • Compute in the truncated ring (Warning 12.7); the arithmetic is Frobenius and Lucas (Exercise 12.10).

  • w1w_1w1​ detects orientability; the mod-222 theory is blind to signs and to odd torsion (Remark 12.12), which is why Chapter 26 exists.
