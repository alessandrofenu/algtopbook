Chapter 24

Sullivan models

In this chapter

  1. 24.1 Commutative models
  2. 24.2 Relative models and fibrations
  3. 24.3 Spatial realisation

Rationally, a simply connected space is a piece of algebra. This chapter makes that precise: to every space one associates a commutative differential graded algebra, and for a large class of spaces a particularly economical model of that algebra — the minimal Sullivan model — determines the rational homotopy type completely, with the rational homotopy groups readable off its generators.

Chapter 19 promised that the parity distinguishing even from odd spheres would reappear as a feature of such a model. Example 24.5 delivers it, and the reader should compare that example with Theorem 19.10 before going further.

24.1 Commutative models

Theorem 24.1 (Polynomial de Rham theory; recalled). There is a functor APLA_{\mathrm{PL}}APL​ from spaces to commutative differential graded algebras over Q\QQQ, with a natural isomorphism

H∗(APL(X)) ≅ H∗(X;Q)H^*\big(A_{\mathrm{PL}}(X)\big)\ \cong\ H^*(X;\QQ)H∗(APL​(X)) ≅ H∗(X;Q)

of graded algebras. Two spaces are rationally equivalent if their APLA_{\mathrm{PL}}APL​’s are connected by a chain of quasi-isomorphisms.

The point is that APL(X)A_{\mathrm{PL}}(X)APL​(X) is commutative on the nose, unlike the singular cochain algebra. Over Q\QQQ that rigidity is available, and everything below exploits it.

Definition 24.2. A Sullivan algebra is a cdga of the form (ΛV,d)(\Lambda V,d)(ΛV,d), free as a graded commutative algebra, whose space of generators admits an increasing filtration

V(0)⊆V(1)⊆⋯ ,V=⋃kV(k),V(0)\subseteq V(1)\subseteq\cdots,\qquad V=\bigcup_kV(k),V(0)⊆V(1)⊆⋯,V=k⋃​V(k),

with d(V(k))⊆Λ(V(k−1))d\big(V(k)\big)\subseteq\Lambda\big(V(k-1)\big)d(V(k))⊆Λ(V(k−1)). It is minimal if in addition d(V)⊆Λ≥2Vd(V)\subseteq\Lambda^{\ge2}Vd(V)⊆Λ≥2V: the differential of a generator has no linear term.

Warning 24.3 (Free is not the condition). Being free as a graded commutative algebra is only half of Definition 24.2. The filtration is the other half, and it is the half that matters: it says the generators can be adjoined in an order such that each new differential is expressible in the ones already present. Without it there is no induction to run, and every argument in this chapter is an induction over that filtration.

A useful consequence to keep in hand: if all generators sit in degree one, then d(V(0))⊆Λ(V(−1))=Qd(V(0))\subseteq\Lambda(V(-1))=\QQd(V(0))⊆Λ(V(−1))=Q, which has nothing in degree two, so ddd vanishes on V(0)V(0)V(0) — some generator must be a cocycle. Any candidate algebra in which no generator is closed therefore fails to be Sullivan, however free it may be.

Theorem 24.4 (Existence and uniqueness; recalled). Every simply connected cdga of finite type admits a minimal Sullivan model, unique up to isomorphism: a quasi-isomorphism (ΛV,d)→A(\Lambda V,d)\to A(ΛV,d)→A with (ΛV,d)(\Lambda V,d)(ΛV,d) minimal. For a simply connected space XXX of finite Q\QQQ-type, the minimal model of APL(X)A_{\mathrm{PL}}(X)APL​(X) is called the minimal model of XXX.

Example 24.5 (Spheres, and the promise of Chapter 19). Let n≥2n\ge2n≥2.

nnn odd. Take (Λ(x),0)(\Lambda(x),0)(Λ(x),0) with ∣x∣=n|x|=n∣x∣=n. Since nnn is odd, x2=0x^2=0x2=0 automatically, so the algebra is Q{1,x}\QQ\{1,x\}Q{1,x} with zero differential and its cohomology is H∗(Sn;Q)H^*(S^n;\QQ)H∗(Sn;Q). It is minimal, ddd being zero.

nnn even. Now (Λ(x),0)(\Lambda(x),0)(Λ(x),0) will not do: xxx has even degree, so Λ(x)=Q[x]\Lambda(x)=\QQ[x]Λ(x)=Q[x] is a polynomial algebra with x2≠0x^2\neq0x2=0, whereas H2n(Sn;Q)=0H^{2n}(S^n;\QQ)=0H2n(Sn;Q)=0. The remedy is to adjoin a second generator to kill x2x^2x2:

(Λ(x,y), dx=0, dy=x2),∣x∣=n, ∣y∣=2n−1.\big(\Lambda(x,y),\ dx=0,\ dy=x^2\big),\qquad |x|=n,\ |y|=2n-1 .(Λ(x,y), dx=0, dy=x2),∣x∣=n, ∣y∣=2n−1.

The degree of yyy is forced — ddd raises degree by one and ∣x2∣=2n|x^2|=2n∣x2∣=2n.

Exercise 24.6 (easy). Verify that the algebra of Example 24.5 for nnn even has the cohomology of SnS^nSn, and that it is minimal.

Hint. yyy has odd degree, so y2=0y^2=0y2=0 and every element is p(x)+q(x)yp(x)+q(x)yp(x)+q(x)y. Compute ddd of such an element.

Solution. Since ∣y∣|y|∣y∣ is odd, y2=0y^2=0y2=0 and the algebra has basis {xk, xky}\{x^k,\ x^ky\}{xk, xky}. The differential is determined by dx=0dx=0dx=0, dy=x2dy=x^2dy=x2 and the Leibniz rule:

d(xk)=0,d(xky)=xk+2.d\big(x^k\big)=0,\qquad d\big(x^ky\big)=x^{k+2}.d(xk)=0,d(xky)=xk+2.

So the cocycles are spanned by the xkx^kxk, and the coboundaries by x2,x3,…x^{2},x^{3},\dotsx2,x3,…. Hence

H∗(Λ(x,y),d)=Q{1,x}=Q[x]/(x2),H^*\big(\Lambda(x,y),d\big)=\QQ\{1,x\}=\QQ[x]/(x^2),H∗(Λ(x,y),d)=Q{1,x}=Q[x]/(x2),

which is H∗(Sn;Q)H^*(S^n;\QQ)H∗(Sn;Q) for nnn even. Minimality holds because dy=x2∈Λ≥2dy=x^2\in\Lambda^{\ge2}dy=x2∈Λ≥2 and dx=0dx=0dx=0, so no differential has a linear term.

Remark 24.7 (The promise, kept). Compare Example 24.5 with Theorem 19.10. For nnn odd the model has one generator, in degree nnn; for nnn even it has two, in degrees nnn and 2n−12n-12n−1. Those are exactly the degrees in which π∗(Sn)⊗Q\pi_*(S^n)\otimes\QQπ∗​(Sn)⊗Q is nonzero.

That is not a coincidence but the content of Theorem 24.16 below: the generators of the minimal model are dual to the rational homotopy groups. So the extra rational homotopy class of an even sphere, which Warning 19.5 detected as an extra generator in the cohomology of a loop space and Remark 19.9 identified as a Whitehead square, is here simply the generator yyy that had to be adjoined to kill x2x^2x2. Three descriptions of one phenomenon, and the algebraic one is the shortest.

24.2 Relative models and fibrations

Definition 24.8. A relative Sullivan algebra over a cdga BBB is a cdga of the form (B⊗ΛV,d)(B\otimes\Lambda V,d)(B⊗ΛV,d) with VVV filtered as in Definition 24.2 and d(V(k))⊆B⊗Λ(V(k−1))d(V(k))\subseteq B\otimes\Lambda(V(k-1))d(V(k))⊆B⊗Λ(V(k−1)).

Relative Sullivan algebras are the cofibrations of the model structure on cdgas, and they model fibrations: a fibration F→E→BF\to E\to BF→E→B of simply connected spaces gives rise to a relative Sullivan algebra over a model of BBB, whose quotient by the augmentation ideal of BBB models FFF.

Exercise 24.9 (medium). Let BBB be a cdga with zero differential and let f∈Bf\in Bf∈B be a non-zero-divisor of even degree. Show that

(B⊗Λ(w), dw=f),∣w∣=∣f∣−1,\big(B\otimes\Lambda(w),\ dw=f\big),\qquad |w|=|f|-1,(B⊗Λ(w), dw=f),∣w∣=∣f∣−1,

is quasi-isomorphic to B/(f)B/(f)B/(f).

Hint. ∣w∣|w|∣w∣ is odd, so every element is b0+b1wb_0+b_1wb0​+b1​w. Compute the differential and read off cocycles and coboundaries.

Solution. Since ∣w∣|w|∣w∣ is odd, w2=0w^2=0w2=0 and a general element is b0+b1wb_0+b_1wb0​+b1​w with bi∈Bb_i\in Bbi​∈B. The differential is d(b0+b1w)=±b1fd(b_0+b_1w)=\pm b_1fd(b0​+b1​w)=±b1​f. Hence the cocycles are the elements with b1f=0b_1f=0b1​f=0, which by the non-zero-divisor hypothesis means b1=0b_1=0b1​=0: the cocycles are BBB. The coboundaries are {bf}=(f)\{bf\}=(f){bf}=(f). So the cohomology is B/(f)B/(f)B/(f), and the quotient map is the required quasi-isomorphism.

Warning 24.10 (Cofibrancy is what makes the quotient correct). The statement that the fibre is modelled by Q⊗B(B⊗ΛV)\QQ\otimes_B(B\otimes\Lambda V)Q⊗B​(B⊗ΛV) requires the model to be a relative Sullivan algebra — a cofibration — and not merely some algebra mapping to another. The tensor product is a non-derived functor; applied to a non-cofibrant map it computes the wrong answer, in exactly the way that the strict pushouts of Example 21.1 computed the wrong homotopy type.

This is the same phenomenon as Warning 22.12: the correct object is defined by a universal property, and only cofibrant representatives compute it.

Remark 24.11 (Building a relative model). The recipe for modelling a map B→AB\to AB→A is the one Example 24.5 followed. Adjoin generators to BBB to build a relative Sullivan algebra mapping quasi-isomorphically to AAA: first generators to hit the cohomology of AAA that BBB misses, then generators to kill the relations that BBB imposes and AAA does not, the differential of each new generator being an element already present. Exercise 24.9 is the case where a single relation has to be killed.

Two things are then worth computing for practice: the cohomology of the resulting relative algebra, which must reproduce H∗(A)H^*(A)H∗(A); and the cohomology of its quotient by the augmentation ideal of BBB, which by Warning 24.10 is a model for the fibre of whichever map of spaces the algebra came from. Doing this for a map of polynomial algebras — say one sending a generator to a power of another — is the natural exercise, and the answer should be recognisable: it will be the cohomology of a space met in Chapter 19.

24.3 Spatial realisation

The passage from algebra back to topology is by a simplicial construction.

Definition 24.12. Let Ω∗(Δn)\Omega^*(\Delta^n)Ω∗(Δn) denote Sullivan’s cdga of polynomial differential forms on the standard nnn-simplex. For a cdga AAA, the spatial realisation is the simplicial set

⟨A⟩n=Hom⁡cdga(A, Ω∗(Δn)).\langle A\rangle_n=\Hom_{\mathrm{cdga}}\big(A,\ \Omega^*(\Delta^n)\big).⟨A⟩n​=Homcdga​(A, Ω∗(Δn)).

Proposition 24.13. ⟨A⟩\langle A\rangle⟨A⟩ is a Kan complex.

Proof sketch. The restriction Ω∗(Δn)→Ω∗(Λkn)\Omega^*(\Delta^n)\to\Omega^*(\Lambda^n_k)Ω∗(Δn)→Ω∗(Λkn​) is surjective — polynomial forms extend from a horn to the simplex — so a horn in ⟨A⟩\langle A\rangle⟨A⟩, which is a compatible family of cdga maps into the forms on the faces, extends to a cdga map into the forms on Δn\Delta^nΔn. That is precisely the horn-filling condition of Definition 23.3.□

Remark 24.14. Proposition 24.13 is the third appearance of one argument. In Exercise 23.4 fibrancy of Sing⁡\SingSing came from a retraction of the topological simplex onto a horn; in Theorem 23.5 it came from an algebraic structure allowing faces to be corrected; here it comes from surjectivity of a restriction map. In each case fibrancy is the statement that some extension problem always has a solution.

Example 24.15 (The realisation of a single generator). Let A=(Λ(x),0)A=(\Lambda(x),0)A=(Λ(x),0) with ∣x∣=k≥2|x|=k\ge2∣x∣=k≥2. A cdga map A→Ω∗(Δn)A\to\Omega^*(\Delta^n)A→Ω∗(Δn) is determined by a closed form of degree kkk, so

⟨A⟩n={closed k-forms on Δn},\langle A\rangle_n=\big\{\text{closed }k\text{-forms on }\Delta^n\big\},⟨A⟩n​={closed k-forms on Δn},

a simplicial abelian group; its homotopy is the degree-kkk cohomology of a simplex in each dimension, and ⟨A⟩≃K(Q,k)\langle A\rangle\simeq K(\QQ,k)⟨A⟩≃K(Q,k). The single generator of VVV in degree kkk has produced a space with a single rational homotopy group, in degree kkk.

Theorem 24.16 (Sullivan; recalled). Let XXX be simply connected of finite Q\QQQ-type with minimal model (ΛV,d)(\Lambda V,d)(ΛV,d). Then for every k≥2k\ge2k≥2

πk(X)⊗Q ≅ Hom⁡Q(Vk,Q).\pi_k(X)\otimes\QQ\ \cong\ \Hom_\QQ\big(V^k,\QQ\big).πk​(X)⊗Q ≅ HomQ​(Vk,Q).

Remark 24.17 (What has to be checked, and where minimality enters). Theorem 24.16 is quoted here; proving it in the form πk⟨A⟩≅Hom⁡(Vk,Q)\pi_k\langle A\rangle\cong\Hom(V^k,\QQ)πk​⟨A⟩≅Hom(Vk,Q) is the natural exercise, and the ingredients are all in place: Proposition 24.13 makes ⟨A⟩\langle A\rangle⟨A⟩ fibrant, so its homotopy groups may be computed simplicially; and Example 24.15 is the case of a single generator, which is the base of the induction over the Sullivan filtration.

Minimality is not decoration. Without it a generator may have a linear term in its differential, and such a generator contributes nothing to homotopy while still enlarging VVV; the statement is then simply false. Anyone proving the theorem should locate the step where d(V)⊆Λ≥2Vd(V)\subseteq\Lambda^{\ge2}Vd(V)⊆Λ≥2V is used, and check what the induction produces without it.

Example 24.18 (Spheres, one last time). Apply Theorem 24.16 to Example 24.5. For nnn odd, V=QV=\QQV=Q concentrated in degree nnn, so π∗(Sn)⊗Q\pi_*(S^n)\otimes\QQπ∗​(Sn)⊗Q is Q\QQQ in degree nnn and zero elsewhere. For nnn even, VVV has one dimension in degree nnn and one in degree 2n−12n-12n−1, so the rational homotopy is Q\QQQ in those two degrees.

That is Theorem 19.10, obtained by writing down a two-generator algebra. Compare the effort with the spectral sequence argument sketched in Warning 19.11.

24.4 What to carry forward

  • APLA_{\mathrm{PL}}APL​ makes rational cohomology into a commutative algebra (Theorem 24.1); minimal models exist and are unique (Theorem 24.4).

  • Sullivan means free plus a filtration (Definition 24.2 and Warning 24.3); in degree one the filtration forces some generator to be closed.

  • The minimal models of spheres, and the parity (Example 24.5, Exercise 24.6, and Remark 24.7) — the promise of Chapter 19.

  • Relative Sullivan algebras model fibrations, and the quotient computes the fibre only because they are cofibrant (Definition 24.8 and Warning 24.10); the Koszul complex is the basic step (Exercise 24.9).

  • Rational homotopy is dual to the generators (Theorem 24.16), with minimality essential (Remark 24.17).
