Chapter 4

Homology you can compute: Δ\DeltaΔ-complexes, cellular chains, degree

In this chapter

  1. 4.1 Δ\DeltaΔ-complexes, and the conventions that make them work
  2. 4.2 Cellular chains
  3. 4.3 Degree
  4. 4.4 What to carry forward

Homology is computable in a way that π1\pi_1π1​ is not: for a space presented by cells, the chain complex is finite in each degree and the boundary maps are integer matrices. The price is bookkeeping — orientations, orderings, signs — and the purpose of this chapter is to fix conventions and then run them on enough examples that the reader trusts their own arithmetic.

Two threads start here and run through the book. The first is torsion: it appears as a cokernel, produced by a gluing that wraps a cell around a cycle more than once, and it is the reason homology is not merely a count of holes. The second is degree, which converts a geometric symmetry into an integer, and which will reappear as the cellular boundary, as the Hopf invariant (Chapter 16) and as the transgression (Chapter 19).

4.1 Δ\DeltaΔ-complexes, and the conventions that make them work

Definition 4.1. A Δ\DeltaΔ-complex structure on XXX is a collection of maps σα ⁣:Δn→X\sigma_\alpha\colon\Delta^{n}\to Xσα​:Δn→X, each restricting to a homeomorphism from the interior of Δn\Delta^nΔn onto its image, such that every point of XXX is in exactly one such image, the restriction of each σα\sigma_\alphaσα​ to a face of Δn\Delta^nΔn is one of the maps of the collection, and a set is open iff its preimage under every σα\sigma_\alphaσα​ is open.

The simplicial chain complex has Δn(X)\Delta_n(X)Δn​(X) free abelian on the nnn-simplices and

∂σ=∑i=0n(−1)i σ∣[v0,…,v^i,…,vn].\partial\sigma=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^i\,\sigma|_{[v_0,\dots,\hat v_i,\dots,v_n]} .∂σ=i=0∑n​(−1)iσ∣[v0​,…,v^i​,…,vn​]​.

Everything below depends on the ordering of the vertices of each simplex, so:

Convention. Before computing, write down the vertex ordering of every simplex, and write down which face of which simplex each lower simplex is. The overwhelming majority of errors in this subject are made before any homology is computed, by counting simplices whose identifications have not been recorded.

Torsion is what a gluing produces when it wraps around with the wrong orientation.

Exercise 4.3 (easy). Compute H∗(RP2;Z)H_*(\RP^2;\ZZ)H∗​(RP2;Z) from the CW structure that has a single cell in dimensions 000, 111 and 222, with the 222-cell attached along a2a^2a2. Hint. For the second half: RP2\RP^2RP2 is the square with edge word abababababab. Follow the four corners around and see which of them are identified.

Solution. The cellular complex is Z→ 2 Z→ 0 Z\ZZ\xrightarrow{\,2\,}\ZZ\xrightarrow{\,0\,}\ZZZ2​Z0​Z, by Example 4.7 with n=2n=2n=2. For the Δ\DeltaΔ-structure, cut the square along a diagonal. Going round the boundary, the corners alternate between two classes, so two vertices survive, not one — unlike the torus and the Klein bottle of Example 4.2, where all four corners were identified. That is exactly the kind of fact that must be recorded before any boundary map is written, and missing it is the standard way to get a wrong answer with a plausible shape.

Remark 4.4. The parenthetical in that solution is not an evasion, it is the moral of the section. Δ\DeltaΔ-complexes are the right setting for defining simplicial homology and for proving it agrees with singular homology, and they are a poor setting for computing, because the number of simplices grows quickly and each one carries an ordering. The next section replaces them.

4.2 Cellular chains

So the boundary map is a matrix of degrees, and computing homology of a CW complex is computing degrees of maps between spheres. Two model cases carry most of the weight.

4.3 Degree

Definition 4.13. For f ⁣:Sn→Snf\colon S^n\to S^nf:Sn→Sn with n≥1n\ge1n≥1, the degree deg⁡f\deg fdegf is the integer with f∗(z)=(deg⁡f) zf_*(z)=(\deg f)\,zf∗​(z)=(degf)z for zzz a generator of Hn(Sn)≅ZH_n(S^n)\cong\ZZHn​(Sn)≅Z; the definition is independent of the generator.

We use without proof the standard properties: degree is a homotopy invariant and detects homotopy classes (f≃g  ⟺  deg⁡f=deg⁡gf\simeq g\iff\deg f=\deg gf≃g⟺degf=degg); deg⁡(fg)=deg⁡f⋅deg⁡g\deg(fg)=\deg f\cdot\deg gdeg(fg)=degf⋅degg; the antipodal map of SnS^nSn has degree (−1)n+1(-1)^{n+1}(−1)n+1; and a map with a regular value having finitely many preimages has degree the sum of the local degrees.

4.4 What to carry forward

  • Fix orderings and identifications before computing (Definition 4.1 and the convention after it); the standard error is made at that stage.

  • Torsion is a cokernel produced by an orientation-reversing gluing (Example 4.2).

  • The cellular boundary is a matrix of degrees (Proposition 4.6); RPn\RP^nRPn (Example 4.7) and the lens spaces (Example 4.8) are the two model computations.

  • Homology fails on lens spaces exactly where π1\pi_1π1​ failed (Remark 4.9) — the second invariant of the book to run out, and the reason for Chapter 7.

  • Euler characteristic as a free consistency check (Exercise 4.12).

  • The double cover multiplies the top class by 222 in odd dimensions and kills it in even ones (Exercise 4.15).
